## Waiting for the owner

- **PENDING (not decided; raised 2026-11-18): the factorization clause of `Ran.CollisionCategory`.** The owner chooses option (a) or option (b) below. Claude and the fresh reviewer recommend (a). Codex has not checked this decision. Nothing in the repository has changed for it yet.
  - **The object.** `Ran.CollisionCategory` (`plans/GLCinLean/AlgebraicGeometry/Ran/CollisionCategory.toml`, a planned definition, not yet in Lean) is Raskin's category C_{X^I}, for a symmetric monoidal DG category C, a smooth curve X and a finite set I ([RaskinCPSI] §§6.5–6.14). It is a limit of the categories Dmod(U(p)) ⊗ C^{⊗K} over pairs of surjections I ↠ J ↠ K. The node assumes nothing on C beyond "presentable, with a tensor product continuous in each variable".
  - **The clause in question.** The node's list of properties contains: "the factorization functors C_{X^{I_1}} ⊗ C_{X^{I_2}} → C_{X^{I_1 ⊔ I_2}}, equivalences over the disjoint locus (§6.10)". The disjoint locus is the open part of X^{I_1 ⊔ I_2} where no point of the first group equals a point of the second. So the node claims this equivalence **for every C**. The source claims the same: §6.10 comes before the assumption "compactly generated and rigid" of §6.15.
  - **The problem.** The whole proof in the source is one sentence: "That this map is an equivalence over the disjoint locus follows from a cofinality argument" (`cpsi.tex` line 1811). The functor is built "in passage to the limit" (line 1805), so it is a composite of two functors. The first goes from (lim C_{Σ_1}) ⊗ (lim C_{Σ_2}) to the limit of the C_{Σ_1} ⊗ C_{Σ_2}. The cofinality argument treats only the second. The first is an equivalence only if the tensor product of DG categories commutes with these two limits, and it does not commute with limits in general. The fresh reviewer found this (`notes/reviews/ae4f745.md`, point 1, verdict *unsure*); Claude read §6.10 and agrees.
  - **What is known.** For a compactly generated rigid C, the missing step is [RaskinCPSI] Lemma 6.18.1, which is already the plan node `Ran.collisionCategory_tensorLimitComparison_isEquivalence`. Its proof (Appendix A.3, induction on |I|) uses factorization for smaller sets, so one induction proves both statements. For a C that is not rigid, the source has no proof, and no other source is known here. The reviewer has a sketch that suggests the clause is still true (the fibre product of Appendix A.3 is a gluing along the small diagonal, and such a gluing commutes with every tensor product); nobody has checked the sketch.
  - **Who uses what.** Every user in `plans/` and `plans-proof/` has a compactly generated rigid C: Rep(H) (`Groups.representationCategory_compactlyGenerated_rigid`), A-mod for a commutative algebra A, and QCoh of affine schemes. The proofs also use a second identification: over each stratum X^J_disj, the category C_{X^I} is C^{⊗J} ⊗ Dmod(X^J_disj). This one needs no assumption on C, because scalar extension to an open or to a closed subscheme commutes with limits. The proof of Theorem 6.17.1 uses only this form (`cpsi.tex` lines 1918–1922), and so can [RaskinCPSII] Proposition A.4.1.
  - **Option (a): claim the equivalence only for compactly generated rigid C** (recommended).
    - Change: in `Ran.CollisionCategory`, keep for every C the factorization functors themselves and add the identification over each stratum; move the claim "equivalences over the disjoint locus" to `Ran.collisionCategory_tensorLimitComparison_isEquivalence`, under its hypothesis; say in the node that the source states more, and why the plan does not. The exact replacement text is the first fix of gap 11 in `notes/reviews/ae4f745.md` (line 74).
    - Advantages: every claim of the plan has a known proof; no user loses anything; no new node is needed.
    - Disadvantages: the node does not read exactly as §6.10; a later user with a non-rigid C must first prove the general claim.
  - **Option (b): keep the claim for every C and take on its proof.**
    - Change: the clause stays; the node says that the source does not prove it for a non-rigid C and that the proof is an obligation of the node, with the reviewer's sketch as a possible route.
    - Advantages: the node matches the source's wording, and the claim has its widest form.
    - Disadvantages: the plan takes on mathematics whose truth is not known. The node is below the statement's own chain (`Ran.CollisionCategory` → `Ran.Representations.equivCollisionLimit` → `Ran.Representations.linearFunctorEquivComodule` → `Satake.naiveFactorizationFunctor` → `Hecke.action`), so a claim that is hard or false there blocks the Hecke action of the statement. Nothing uses the extra generality.
  - **Why (a).** (1) `AGENTS.md` asks that library code be real mathematics; a claim with no known proof, on the path to the statement, is a risk with no benefit. (2) `AGENTS.md` allows no abstraction that nothing uses, and the general claim has no user. (3) Option (a) does not weaken the statement: the rule against weakening protects the main theorem and the definitions it reaches, the theorem uses C = Rep(Ǧ), which is rigid, and the object C_{X^I} itself keeps its full generality; only an unused case of one property is not claimed. A property stated more narrowly than its source is allowed for a lemma that still proves what it is used for. (4) Option (a) can be extended later without rework: a proof for every C is added as a new theorem and no node changes. Option (b) is not undone as cheaply if the proof fails.
  - **A point against (a), for the owner to weigh.** The decision of 2026-11-11 on crystals says that a theorem narrower than its source is under-generalization. There the source proved the general theorem. Here the source's proof has a gap exactly in the general case, so the narrower claim is the part that is proved.
  - **After the decision.** A session applies the chosen text to `Ran/CollisionCategory.toml` (and, for (a), to `Ran/CollisionCategoryTensor.toml`), records the decision under "Decisions taken", and removes this item. The other fixes of the review do not depend on this decision ("Next", item 1).

## Decisions taken

Only the decisions that still guide the work are listed. Every other decision is applied, and the plan, the code and `AGENTS.md` carry it. The full text of all decisions up to 2026-11-18 is in git history: `git show 6183267:STATUS.md`.

- **Generality** (2026-11-11 and 2026-11-13). A definition gets its natural generality. A theorem stated more narrowly than its source is under-generalization. A theorem that serves only a proof is stated under the hypotheses its proof uses, with the case used as an instance.
- **The ∞-categorical layer** (2026-11-10). Build the enhanced theory on Mathlib quasicategories and Tau Ceti's DG nerve and K-injective models, following the accepted EnhancedDerivedSheaves and BordismFormalGroups targets. Do not start a competing foundation, and do not replace enhanced categories by triangulated ones. The constructible category is the exception by design: an ordinary triangulated category built from Mathlib (2026-11-13).
- **Open Tau Ceti roadmap pull requests** (2026-11-10). Use them as design evidence, with their exact head recorded. The accepted roadmap stays the authority until a proposal merges. Never import a proposal's `Suggested.lean` or pin this project to an unmerged branch.
- **Other Lean libraries** (2026-11-10). Read-only discovery and reuse of ideas under compatible licences. Adding a dependency first needs a list of the declarations used, compatible Lean and Mathlib versions, a licence check, an axiom audit and a comparison with the existing objects. AxiomMath Stacks and Weil are prior art for Worker ports, case by case.
- **Names** (2026-11-13). Category-, scheme- and prestack-valued constructions and predicates get UpperCamelCase names, as the statement's `QCohLS`, `DmodHalf` and `RepRan` do. A node that carries data a later definition uses is a definition. Roadmap-owned nodes keep their ids, which Tau Ceti fixes.
- **2026-11-18.** The `Setting` refactor is approved and merged (`ecbb930`). For the comparison of the two definitions of `Rep(Ǧ)_Ran`: the group H is every affine group scheme of finite type, and the collision category, its ULA and tensor-limit theorems, the regular object and the general ULA nodes are plan nodes (plan commit `ae4f745`).
