## Upstream declarations the plan uses

These exist. The node that uses one names it and its module in its `desc`. The note says where it falls short of the paper's object. All are at the pin: on 2026-11-26 `python3 tools/check-upstream-names.py` confirmed each pair of a declaration and its module that `plans/`, `plans-proof/` and this file name as declared in that module in the pinned source; the header of the script states its limits. The check is a scan of the source text that follows the namespaces, because the main checkout builds only the Tau Ceti modules that `GLCinLean` imports. Tau Ceti moves declarations between modules without a notice (a file becomes a folder with a module `Basic`), so this check is run at every pin move. The rows marked "(no node names it yet)" supply nodes that the plan still plans.

## Roadmap candidates (deferred)

The owner deferred on 2026-11-11 the decision to write or submit Tau Ceti roadmaps for the parts of the plan that no roadmap owns. The candidates are recorded here so that a later decision can rest on the state of this project, of Tau Ceti and of the roadmaps at that time. Each audit (`prompts/audit.md`) checks the triggers below and updates the column "State"; the decision is the owner's.

A candidate is put to the owner only when all four triggers hold:

1. Tau Ceti main has merged code, not only roadmap text, for the roadmaps in its column "Rests on".
2. No accepted roadmap or open roadmap proposal covers its scope.
3. Its nodes are what blocks the next work here: no module under `GLCinLean` is ready apart from modules that it would own.
4. A reviewer who knows the subject is known.

How Tau Ceti roadmaps are shaped, as read on 2026-11-11 from the TauCetiRoadmap README and `CONTRIBUTING.md`:

- A roadmap covers a whole subject with its full basic theory, not only what one project needs. So each candidate is larger than the plan nodes it would take.
- It has one arXiv subject class (`metadata.toml`), a `README.md` that is the specification, and a `Suggested.lean` file that builds against the pinned Mathlib and Tau Ceti.
- Its lowest step must rest on material that is already formalized.
- A large subject is split into parts ("Part I", "Part II").
- Merged roadmaps near this plan have 300 to 9,000 lines of README: small (FlagVarieties, NilpotentOrbits, JacobianChallenge), medium (AffineLieAlgebras, about 1,200), large (EnhancedDerivedSheaves, DerivedModuleAlgebra, BordismFormalGroups, about 3,000), very large (ReductiveGroups, SchemeAndStackFoundations).
- A proposal may be written with AI help: the pull request names the models, and the person who submits it has read it. It merges when one member of the roadmap-reviewers team approves it and the build passes. A proposal that cites an unmerged proposal waits with the label `awaiting-dependencies`.
- An agent never opens a roadmap pull request or issue (Tau Ceti `AGENTS.md`); the owner submits.

## Limits of existing providers

- The Tannakian reconstruction API of Tau Ceti starts from a given Hopf algebra. The reconstruction of the group of a neutral Tannakian category, and the criteria for finite type, connectedness and closed immersions, are roadmap nodes of MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles §§6.1–6.3 (`plans/Roadmap/MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles/`); Tau Ceti has their inputs and their statements for a given Hopf algebra, and not the reconstruction (the nodes say which). That the spherical heart satisfies the hypotheses is written here: `Satake.cohomologyFiberFunctor`, `Satake.sphericalHeartDual` and `Satake.tannakaGroup`. The roadmap states its headline `neutralTannakian_recognition` with the Hopf algebra under an existential quantifier and takes rigidity as a hypothesis. It gives the coalgebra its bialgebra structure before it uses rigidity (§6.2, and §6.4 under its relative hypotheses), but it does not state [DeligneMilne] Proposition 1.20, which derives rigidity from the invertibility of the objects with a fibre of dimension 1.
- The roadmap nodes on D-modules (SpringerResolutionAndCharacteristicCycles Layers 8–9) are for left modules on smooth separated schemes of finite type of pure dimension over a field of characteristic 0, on ordinary bounded derived categories. They have no singular schemes, no stacks, no crystals and no enhanced categories; their equivalence of Kashiwara is for a closed immersion of smooth schemes, and the plan's (`DModules.extraordinaryPullback_isEquivalence_of_isClosedImmersion`) is for prestacks; the text of the roadmap does not state that direct and inverse image along a general map preserve holonomic or regular holonomic complexes (for regular holonomic complexes the plan has two roadmap nodes that are prerequisites: `DModules.regularHolonomicDModule_directImage_inverseImage_complex`, which the accepted roadmap MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory requests, and `DModules.regularHolonomicDModule_directImage_inverseImage`, its extension to every field of characteristic 0); and the roadmap states that the general regular holonomic Riemann–Hilbert correspondence is not part of it. The enhanced functors on crystals and their comparisons with the classical functors (clauses of `DModules.rightCrystals_equiv_dModules`, `DModules.deRhamPushforward` and `DModules.verdierDuality`, and for the external product a proof obligation of `DModules.IsRegularHolonomic`) and the theorems that the roadmap does not state are written here; the generic enhanced operations on derived categories of sheaves of modules are supplied by EnhancedDerivedSheaves (`Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategory`, `Infinity.derivedBimoduleTransfer`).
- The roadmap nodes on constructible complexes and on the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence (MixedHodgeModulesAndIrregularHodgeTheory Layer 1) are over ℂ, on ordinary derived categories, with coefficients ℚ and ℂ in the roadmap; the correspondence is for left modules on smooth schemes. They have no scheme with singularities on the side of the D-modules, no crystal, no named target for the complexes along a fixed stratification, and no enhanced category. Because triangulated categories do not glue along an open cover, the de Rham functor written here (`DModules.analyticDeRham`) is for the schemes that are closed in a smooth scheme. A scheme that has no such embedding needs the enhanced form of `D^b_c` and of the functor, which no accepted roadmap states.
- A local or p-adic Satake roadmap is not a de Rham provider.
- A planned theorem is not evidence that its proof source is complete.
