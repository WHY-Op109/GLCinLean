## 1. The problem

The node `LoopGroups.LoopGroup` (module `GLCinLean.AlgebraicGeometry.LoopGroups.Basic`) reads on `main`:

> For an affine group scheme H of finite type over k, the loop group and arc group functors R ↦ H(R((t))) and R ↦ H(R[[t]]), their ind-scheme and pro-scheme presentations, and the action of coordinate changes; families over Ran use the formal discs of Ran.FormalDisc for coordinate independence. No reductivity or pinning is assumed. Provider: here.

Its sources are [BDHitchin] §4.5, [MV] §2 and [GLys] §§7.1–7.2. It depends on `Ran.FormalDisc`, `Derived.Prestack` and `Derived.IsIndscheme`. Seven nodes of `plans/` and three nodes of `plans-proof/` depend on it.

The node is under "Held" in `STATUS.md`, in one row with nine nodes on the affine Grassmannian, because the open roadmap pull request TauCetiRoadmap #1734 (SheafTheoreticSmithTheory) would own the loop Grassmannian. Finding B5 of `notes/audit-2026-11-18.md` says that an accepted roadmap, FormalConnectionsOpers, already owns the loop group. The question is who provides the loop group, and what of the node stays here.

## 2. The evidence

### 2.1 The accepted roadmap FormalConnectionsOpers

Read at TauCetiRoadmap main `fe56342a` (2026-11-19). Its `README.md` and `Suggested.lean` are the same files as in the copy of 2026-11-18 that the audit used. The area has no generated `STATUS.md`.

- **Base ring.** "Conventions": "Work over a characteristic-zero field `k` for the remaining algebraic and formal calculus", and "The moduli and Drinfeld–Sokolov constructions use every commutative `k`-algebra `R`, including nonreduced algebras, rather than only field-valued points." `Suggested.lean` states the coefficient maps `positiveLoopToFormal` and `polynomialLoopToFormal` for a commutative Hopf algebra over any commutative ring `k`. So the stated case is a field of characteristic zero with all test algebras, and the roadmap itself generalizes two maps beyond it.
- **§3.1 (F3.1), "Formal loop groups and congruence subgroups".** "For the native commutative coordinate Hopf algebra `H` of an affine group `G`, define `LG(R) = G(F_R)`, `L⁺G(R) = G(O_R)` and `K_N(R) = ker(G(O_R) → G(R[t]/t^N))` for `N ≥ 1`", with `F_R = R((t))` and `O_R = R[[t]]`, on `TauCeti.HopfAlgebra.points`. "Construct their functors and maps, congruence inclusions, normality of `K_N` in `L⁺G`, separation `∩_N K_N = {1}`, the proalgebraic presentation of `L⁺G` by jets, and the inverse-limit description of `K_N`." Its bullets: "`LG`, `L⁺G` and `K_N` are group functors on commutative `k`-algebras, with `K_(N+1) ⊂ K_N` normal in `L⁺G` and `L⁺G/K_N ≃ G(R[t]/t^N)`". In characteristic zero it adds the exponential `exp_N` of `K_N` with the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff law. The class of groups is every affine group; no reductivity.
- **§3.2 (F3.2), "Ind-affine presentation and coefficient descent".** "Construct `LG` as an ind-affine functor by bounding poles in a finite presentation of `H` and of the inverse point. Prove the presentation's independence of embedding and cofinal bounds, functoriality and the fpqc sheaf condition, including nonreduced `R`." Its bullets: "The pole-bounded subfunctors `LG_(≤N)` are affine, `LG = colim_N LG_(≤N)`", and "`LG` is an fpqc sheaf on commutative `k`-algebras". So the groups are those of finite presentation, and "ind-scheme" means a functor on commutative `k`-algebras that is a union of affine subfunctors. §3.2 does not say that the inclusions of the pieces are closed embeddings; they are, because the next piece is cut out by the vanishing of one more coefficient.
- **§1.2 (F1.2), "Coordinate automorphisms".** "Define coordinate automorphisms by `φ ∈ tR[[t]]` with unit coefficient of `t`. Construct the inverse substitution recursively, the group functor `Aut(O)`, its composition order, and its action on `F_R` and on differential forms." "Substitution is a continuous `R`-algebra automorphism of `F_R` preserving `O_R`". "Scope and ownership" says that the roadmap owns "the relative formal calculus over arbitrary coefficient algebras (Layer 1)". The action on loops is not in §1.2. §6.3 orders it as a construction, on ℂ-points: "Convert §1.2's right substitution to a left action `ρ_φ = σ_(φ⁻¹)` on coefficients and Hopf-valued loops. Use the semidirect product `(g, φ)(h, ψ) = (g ρ_φ(h), φ∘ψ)`". §5.1 constructs a right action of `Aut(O)(R)` on opers "natural in `R`". It does not name the action on loops; that it needs one is an inference from the gauge action.
- **Layer 3A.** §3A.5 constructs the loop Grassmannian and the affine flag variety "as ind-schemes over `ℂ`", for a connected reductive complex group. It is not a provider for the plan's field.
- **The form of the objects.** Functors from commutative `k`-algebras to groups, and unions of affine subfunctors. The roadmap has no derived rings and no prestacks with values in spaces. Its loop groups are those of the disc with the coordinate `t`. A curve occurs once: §5.1 says "Prove coordinate independence by gluing this action over the torsor of formal coordinates on a smooth curve", for opers. It has no loop group at several points of a curve and none over the Ran space.

### 2.5 The papers

- [GLC2] Appendix B is the source for the family over the Ran space. §B.1.1: the Ran space has as S-points the finite nonempty subsets of `Hom(S_red, X)`. §B.3.1: the formal disc is the completion of `S × X` along the closed subset of the graphs. §B.3.2: a prestack is disjoint-union-local if it sends a disjoint union of two affine schemes to the product. §§B.3.3 and B.3.11: the discs of two disjoint points. §B.3.4: the affine disc, and the extension of the map from the formal disc to `X` to the affine disc (a theorem of Bhatt, with a direct argument in Remark B.3.5). Remark B.3.6: the formation of the affine disc is not compatible with Zariski localization on `S`. §B.3.7: the punctured disc. §B.1.4: the test schemes over the Ran space are tacitly of finite type. §§B.4.1–B.4.2 and B.4.6: horizontal arcs and loops, with factorization for a disjoint-union-local target. Lemma B.4.8: the arcs are an affine scheme, the loops an ind-affine ind-scheme, and the inclusion a closed embedding into a piece. §B.4.10: the Grassmannian over the Ran space by the discs, the projection from the loop group, and the étale quotient. §§B.5.4–B.5.5: arcs and loops into an affine scheme over `X` are the maps from the disc and from the punctured disc. Example B.5.6: the pieces for an affine space, by the multiples of the divisor of lifted graphs.
- [GLC2] §§1.1.2 and 1.1.14 use the group ind-scheme `𝔏(G)` over the Ran space and the multiplicative gerbe on it, the pullback of the gerbe on the Grassmannian.
- [GLys] works with a connected algebraic group. §1.2.1: a prestack is a functor on the classical affine schemes. §2.1.1: its Ran space has as S-points the finite nonempty subsets of `Maps(S, X)`. §§7.1.1–7.1.4: the discs, `𝔏⁺(Z)` and `𝔏(Z)`. §7.1.3 says that they are factorization prestacks for every prestack `Z`; this is false without the condition of [GLC2] §B.3.2 (§7 below). §§7.2.1–7.2.2: Beauville–Laszlo by the discs, the action of `𝔏(G)` on the Grassmannian with stabilizer `𝔏⁺(G)` of the unit section, the quotient, and the ind-scheme property of the loop group over a test scheme.
- [MV] §2 says that the group scheme Aut(O) acts on the loop group, on the arc group and on the affine Grassmannian; its Proposition 2.2 says that the spherical perverse sheaves are equivariant for it.
- [DGIndschemes] §1.1: an n-coconnective ind-scheme, and a classical ind-scheme for n = 0. Lemma 1.4.7 and §1.4.8: an n-coconnective ind-scheme gives a DG ind-scheme, fully faithfully, by the convergent completion of the colimit of its pieces taken as derived schemes; this is the convergent completion of the fppf sheafification of the left Kan extension, and the left Kan extension alone need not be a sheaf. §9.2.1 defines the loop space on derived affine schemes; Proposition 9.2.4 says that it is a DG ind-scheme for an affine target almost of finite type; Theorem 9.3.5 says that the loop group of an algebraic group is 0-coconnective.

**The relation to the plan's prestacks.** `Derived.Prestack` has, as part of its API, the restriction to classical affine test schemes (classical prestacks). A functor from commutative `k`-algebras to sets is a classical prestack with values in sets. So the comparison is short: the roadmap's functors, seen as group objects of classical prestacks. Gerbes on a prestack are tested on classical affine schemes (`Stacks.EtaleGerbe`), and every category of D-modules depends only on the reduced classical affine schemes (`Derived.DeRhamPrestack`).

**The relation to `Derived.IsIndscheme`.** The predicate is about a convergent derived prestack and asks a presentation at every n, so it cannot be stated of a functor on classical rings. `Derived.IsIndscheme` now names its stage at each n, with the classical ind-scheme at n = 0, and the fully faithful extension from a stage to a derived ind-scheme: the convergent completion of the colimit of the pieces, taken as derived schemes, in the prestacks on all derived affine schemes ([DGIndschemes] §1.1, Lemma 1.4.7, §1.4.8; both are inside the sources that the node already cites). At a positive n the pieces form a filtered colimit along closed embeddings, and a union only at n = 0. `LoopGroups.LoopGroup` and `GLCinLean.AffineGrassmannian` state the stage n = 0 for the prestack. `LoopGroups.RanLoopGroup` states it for the base change of the loop group to each test scheme over the Ran space; the total prestack over the Ran space is not an ind-scheme. A node that needs a derived prestack, for example a category of D-modules on the Grassmannian, applies the extension. The values of a loop group on derived affine schemes that are not classical ([DGIndschemes] §9.2) are not part of any node: no node of `plans/` uses them.

**The relation to `Ran.FormalDisc` and to the two Ran spaces.** `Ran.FormalDisc` is the formal completion. The loop group over Ran needs two more objects, the affine disc and the punctured disc; they are part (1) of `LoopGroups.RanLoopGroup`. Part (1) also has the map from the affine disc to `S × X` that extends the map from the formal disc ([GLC2] §B.3.4); the definition of the loop group into a constant target does not use it, and `Hecke.loopGroupAction` uses it to restrict bundles to the discs. The discs depend only on the closed subset of the graphs. So the node defines the family over `Ran(X_dR)`, whose S-points are the finite sets of maps `S_red → X` ([GLC2] §B.1.1; the base that the statement uses, `GLCinLean.Ran`), and the families over `Ran(X)` ([GLys]) and over `X^I` are its pullbacks. The action groupoids and the gerbes over the base of the statement are then transported by pullback, with no further comparison.

## 5. The options

**(a) Split: the classical loop group goes to the roadmap, the rest stays here.** Two roadmap nodes under `plans/Roadmap/FormalConnectionsOpers/` (the functors with their presentations; the coordinate changes). Here: the statements in terms of prestacks, and the family over Ran. Cost: new nodes; one more roadmap area to follow; one author worker. Risk: Tau Ceti's review may decline the action on loops, or a generality beyond a field of characteristic zero. The first has a stated way back; the second is not a condition of the plan. It follows "Sources of code": the covered part goes to Tau Ceti, and no second copy is written here. It follows "Lean code": each concept has its own module.

- **(a1)** one node here for the whole remainder.
- **(a2)** two nodes here: `LoopGroups.LoopGroup` (the local prestacks, same id and module) and `LoopGroups.RanLoopGroup` (the family over Ran, a new module). The five local users then do not wait for `Ran.FormalDisc`, and each module has one concept.

**(b) The node stays here.** Cost: none now. It breaks "Sources of code": the functors `R ↦ H(R((t)))` with their presentations are a target of an accepted roadmap, so writing them here is a second copy that competes with it. Rejected.

**(c) Wait for #1734.** Cost: the seven users wait for a proposal without review that has the labels `awaiting-review` and `awaiting-dependencies`. No outcome of #1734 changes the owner of the loop group (§2.4), and an accepted roadmap is the authority ("Decisions taken", "Open Tau Ceti roadmap pull requests"). Rejected.

**(d) Move the whole node to the roadmap, with the families over Ran as prerequisites.** No target of FormalConnectionsOpers or of another accepted roadmap needs the families over Ran or a prestack on derived rings. Tau Ceti accepts only targets and their genuine prerequisites. Rejected.

**(e) Use the Weil restriction and the congruence subgroups at the pin and write only the loop functor here.** This still writes a roadmap target here. Rejected for the reason of (b).

## 6. The choice

Option (a2). The checker and Codex agree with this division and with the release of the loop group from "Held" (round 1).

- `LoopGroups.FormalLoopGroup` (new, `Roadmap.FormalConnectionsOpers.FormalLoopGroups`): the functors `LG`, `L⁺G`, `K_N`; the tower of the jet groups and its limit; the pole-bounded pieces with closed embeddings; the fpqc sheaf property; checks.
- `LoopGroups.coordinateChange` (new, `Roadmap.FormalConnectionsOpers.CoordinateAutomorphisms`): the substitution automorphisms of `R((t))`, and the left action `ρ_φ = σ_(φ⁻¹)` on loops, with the convention of §6.3.
- For both roadmap nodes the case that the plan needs is a field of characteristic zero, a group of finite type and every test algebra. The wider generality (a commutative ring) is asked where Tau Ceti's review accepts it.
- `LoopGroups.LoopGroup` (same id and module, new text): `LH` and `L⁺H` as group objects of classical prestacks; `L⁺H` an affine scheme and a limit of smooth groups with unipotent kernels; `LH` a classical ind-scheme; the action of `Aut(O)`. It no longer depends on `Ran.FormalDisc`.
- `LoopGroups.RanLoopGroup` (new, `GLCinLean.AlgebraicGeometry.LoopGroups.Ran`): the discs, `𝔏⁺(Z)` and `𝔏(Z)` over `Ran(X_dR)` with their pullbacks to `Ran(X)` and `X^I`, factorization for a disjoint-union-local `Z`, representability over a test scheme, and the comparison with `LoopGroups.LoopGroup` for a point with a relative coordinate, with the formula for a change of coordinate.
- `Hecke.loopGroupAction` (new, `GLCinLean.GeometricLanglands.Hecke.LoopGroupAction`): the Beauville–Laszlo comparison over the Ran space by the discs, the left action of `𝔏(G)` on the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian, the stabilizer of the unit section, and the Grassmannian as the étale quotient by the arc group. Its part (4) has the Beauville–Laszlo comparison at a point with a coordinate, which follows from (1) and (3). `Hecke.Correspondence` no longer asks for that comparison, so that it is asked once and the special case comes from the general one; nothing else of `Hecke.Correspondence` changes. `Satake.fusion`, which uses the fibres of the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian at the points and on the diagonals, now depends on `Hecke.loopGroupAction`. `Hecke.correspondence_pseudoProper` does not: its text uses the ind-properness of the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian over `X^I` and not the comparison at a point, and it is in the module of `Hecke.Correspondence`, so the dependency would be a cycle of modules.
- `Satake.criticalDeterminantGerbe` and `Satake.factorizationSphericalCategory` depend on `LoopGroups.RanLoopGroup` and `Hecke.loopGroupAction` in place of `LoopGroups.LoopGroup`, and name them. In `Satake.criticalDeterminantGerbe` the sentence on [GLC2] Remarks 1.1.4 and 1.1.7 is corrected: the square root of the determinant line bundle exists as a line bundle; its compatibility with factorization holds only with the ℤ/2-grading; so the trivialization of the gerbe is in general not compatible with factorization, and it is when the grading is zero, as for the trivial group.
- `Satake.criticalTwistBunDeterminantEquiv` names the construction of the pullback of the determinant gerbe to the Ran Hecke stack: restriction of a modification to the discs is a map to the quotient of the Grassmannian over Ran by the left action of the arc group, to which the equivariant gerbe descends. It depends on `Hecke.loopGroupAction`.
- `Derived.IsIndscheme` names its stage at each n and the extension from a stage (§3).
- `GLCinLean.AffineGrassmannian` (held, and it stays held) gets two sentences that the split forces: the quotient is formed in classical prestacks, and the action of `Aut(O)` on the loop group induces one on the Grassmannian. The eight other nodes of the held row are unchanged.
- `plans-proof/`: `SpectralProof.sphericalKL` and `SpectralProof.opers_indPlacid` also depend on `LoopGroups.RanLoopGroup`, and `SpectralProof.opers_indPlacid` says that it constructs the loops into its twisted vector bundle.
