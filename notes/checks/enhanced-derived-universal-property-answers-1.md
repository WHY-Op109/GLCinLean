## The point where the two reports differ

Codex: the saving "is real in principle", through "the lax symmetric monoidal dg nerve" of Drew, §2, Theorem 2.4. The checker: no route without a localization exists as items that can be proved. I read the two sources and the pin. The checker is right.

**What Drew states.** B. Drew, "Verdier quotients of stable quasi-categories are localizations", read at `https://bdrew.gitlab.io/pdf/verdier-quotients.pdf` on 2026-11-26: Definition 2.1 (vii), pp. 8–9, and Theorem 2.4, p. 10. The paper is not pinned under `references/papers/`.

- Theorem 2.4 is: "(i) The qcategory dgCat_K[W_DK^{-1}] inherits a symmetric monoidal structure from dgCat_K^⊗. (ii) The functor N_dg underlies a lax symmetric monoidal functor … dgCat_K[W_DK^{-1}]^⊗ → QCat^×. (iii) In particular, N_dg^⊗ induces a functor CAlg(dgCat_K[W_DK^{-1}]^⊗) → CAlg(QCat^×)." It is a statement about functors of quasicategories. Its source is a localization of the category of dg categories at the Dwyer–Kan equivalences. Its proof is one paragraph: "Assertion (i) now follows from [Lur14, 4.1.3.4] and assertion (ii) is explained in [Hin15, 3.2.2]."
- It does not give `SSet.SymmetricMonoidal` as the pin defines it (`TauCeti/AlgebraicTopology/Quasicategory/SymmetricMonoidal.lean`, lines 87–105: a coCartesian fibration over the nerve of `FinPointed` with the Segal condition). It gives a commutative algebra object of the quasicategory of quasicategories. To get a fibration from it one needs the quasicategory of quasicategories with its products, commutative algebra objects in it, and straightening over the nerve of `FinPointed`. None of these is at the pin.
- Definition 2.1 (vii) introduces a second nerve, "a useful variant of N_dg … which will allow us to obtain stable symmetric monoidal qcategories": truncation, the Dold–Kan functor to simplicial modules, the forgetful functor, then the simplicial nerve. It compares this nerve with the dg nerve by Higher Algebra 1.3.1.17. The text that I extracted from the PDF does not keep the bar that tells the two nerves apart, so I cannot say which of them Theorem 2.4 names. Drew calls the three functors "lax symmetric monoidal right adjoint functors". For the Dold–Kan functor this is not true on the level of categories; see the next paragraph.

**What Hinich states.** V. Hinich, "Rectification of algebras and modules", arXiv 1311.4130v7, read on 2026-11-26: §§3.1–3.2, pp. 18–21. Not pinned.

- §3.2.1, p. 20: normalized chains are "lax symmetric monoidal via ▽" (the shuffle map) and "also colax monoidal via △ (equivalently, N_* is lax monoidal via △)", with the footnote "but not colax symmetric monoidal!". So the functor from complexes to simplicial modules, which gives the mapping spaces, is lax monoidal and not symmetric. Tau Ceti says the same of its own simplicial enrichment (`TauCeti/CategoryTheory/DG/Simplicial.lean`, module doc comment: "the Alexander–Whitney lax monoidal functor is not symmetric monoidal").
- §3, p. 18: "we will construct certain SM ∞-categories (of ∞-categories, of dg categories and others) by Dwyer-Kan localization". §3.2.2–3.2.3 get the symmetric structure of the nerve from this: the shuffle functor on enriched categories is lax symmetric monoidal and an equivalence after localization (a Quillen equivalence of Tabuada), and the nerve is its inverse there.
- §3.2.4, p. 21, identifies the result for the complexes with "QC(k)^⊗ as constructed in 3.1.1", which is a symmetric monoidal localization of the complexes at the quasi-isomorphisms, "by universality of symmetric monoidal localization", and uses that the dg nerve is that localization ("It is an equivalence by [H.L], 1.4.3").

So the route that Codex names uses Dwyer–Kan localizations of the category of dg categories and of the category of categories enriched in simplicial modules. These are other theorems than the localization of the complexes at the quasi-isomorphisms, and none of them is at the pin. Hinich compares the result with the localized complexes in a step after the construction (§3.2.4), so the construction itself is not shown to need that theorem. (Round 2 corrected this paragraph.) The route is longer than the roadmap's, not shorter.

**No strict construction on the dg nerve.** The checker's calculation is right, and I had found the first half of it: a natural map from `N_dg(C) × N_dg(D)` to `N_dg(C ⊗ D)` has, on 2-simplices, the form `h_012 = α (f_012 ⊗ g_02 + f_12 f_01 ⊗ g_012) + (1 − α)(f_012 ⊗ g_12 g_01 + f_02 ⊗ g_012)`. The form is forced: by naturality the map is given by its value on the universal 2-simplices, and these four terms span the morphisms of degree −1 there. Symmetry forces `2α = 1`. Associativity forces `α² = α` (the coefficient of `f_012 ⊗ g_02 ⊗ e_02` is `α²` on one side and `α` on the other). So no such map is both associative and symmetric, over any ring. This is what Hinich's footnote says for the mapping spaces. A symmetric monoidal structure on the dg nerve therefore needs either a localization or a full system of higher coherences for the Alexander–Whitney map; no source read here writes the second down, and it is a larger work than the roadmap's route.

**One route without the theorem exists over a field of characteristic 0, and I do not choose it.** With polynomial differential forms on simplices in place of the Dold–Kan functor, the mapping spaces are strictly symmetric monoidal, the simplicial category of operators exists, and integration over cubes gives a strict map from its nerve to the dg nerve. It needs the polynomial forms with their Poincaré lemma, Stokes' formula on cubes, the mapping spaces of a simplicial nerve, and a comparison theorem; it is restricted to characteristic 0, where `Infinity.derivedVect` is stated for every field; and the roadmap names nothing of it. It is longer than the roadmap's route. This is my own sketch and was not checked further.
