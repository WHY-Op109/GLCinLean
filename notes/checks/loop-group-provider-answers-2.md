## Codex

**1. The extension in `Derived.IsIndscheme`.** Fixed. The finding is right: [DGIndschemes] §1.4.8 uses the functor with sheafification, and its introduction says that the left Kan extension alone does not send stacks to stacks. IND now states the explicit construction of §1.4.8: take the pieces as derived schemes, take their colimit in the prestacks on all derived affine schemes, and take the convergent completion; it adds that this is the convergent completion of the fppf sheafification of the left Kan extension and that the left Kan extension alone is not used. The first sentence of IND now says "filtered colimits … along closed embeddings", with the meaning of a closed embedding at a positive n and with "a filtered union" only for n = 0; this is the definition of [DGIndschemes] §1.1. The stage n = 0 for the Ran family is stated for the base change to each test scheme: IND, RAN (4) and PROP §3 say that the total prestack over the Ran space is not an ind-scheme.

**2. The map from the affine disc to the curve, and the Grassmannian as a fibre of the Hecke stack.** Fixed. RAN (1) now has the map `D_x → S × X` that extends the map from the formal disc, with its source ([GLC2] §B.3.4, a theorem of Bhatt, and the direct argument of Remark B.3.5), its restriction from the punctured disc to the complement of the graphs, and the naturality in `S` and the compatibility with disjoint unions. It is placed in the disc construction, the first of the two homes that the finding names, because it is a property of the discs. RAN (2) says that the definition for a constant target does not use it. HLA (1) restricts bundles along it. HLA defines the Grassmannian over the Ran space as the fibre, over the trivial first bundle, of the Hecke stack of `Hecke.Correspondence` over `Ran(X_dR)`, and says that its pullback to `X^I` is the Grassmannian of that node.

## The second reading of the nodes

Every node that the branch adds or rewrites was read again for the ambient category of each construction, the domain and codomain of each map, the source of each input, the quantifiers of each check, and the cases of the zero ring, the trivial group, a finite group, a disconnected test scheme, colliding points and the empty set of points. Points found and fixed:

1. **FLG (4), the index of the pieces.** The text did not say that N is a natural number. It now does, and says that the piece for N = 0 is the arc group and that a piece is in general not a subgroup (for 𝔾_m the product of two points of a piece can leave it).
2. **FLG (5), the zero ring.** "The coordinate x alone does not generate H" and the checks around it are false over the zero ring. The checks are now "over a nonzero ring k".
3. **FLG (4), reducedness.** The text said only "need not be of finite type". With the example of Codex 7 it now also says that a piece need not be reduced.
4. **LG (4), an input without a source.** "Aut(O) is represented by an affine group scheme" had no named input and no owner. The text now says that each jet group is the product of 𝔾_m and an affine space and that this node proves the representability from the coefficients.
5. **RAN (1), an unstated step.** That the punctured disc is affine, and that the arc group maps injectively to the loop group, both use that the divisor `D` is an effective Cartier divisor in the affine disc. The text now states this, with the reason: the ideal is the limit of the sections of the ideal sheaf over the multiples of `D`, an invertible module over a complete ring.
6. **RAN (1), citations.** The failure of base change of the affine disc is [GLC2] Remark B.3.6, not §B.3.4; the divisor of lifted graphs is Example B.5.6; the multiples are for n ≥ 1. [GLC2] §B.1.4 takes test schemes of finite type, and the node says that it states its parts for every classical affine scheme, by the divisor.
7. **RAN (3), a quantifier.** The set J of the factorization isomorphism has at least two elements.
8. **RAN (5), the pieces.** The comparison of the pieces over a test scheme with the pieces of the roadmap holds for the same generators of the ring of `H`; the text now says so.
9. **HLA, the values of the Grassmannian.** The text spoke of groupoids of pairs without saying that a pair has no automorphism. It now says why (the complement of the graphs is schematically dense and `G` is affine), so the Grassmannian has values in sets and the quotient of (3) is a quotient of sheaves of sets. The check for 𝔾_m describes the points directly as line bundles with a trivialization away from the point.
10. **HLA, the base.** The Hecke stack of `Hecke.Correspondence` is named over `Ran(X_dR)`.
11. **PROV, a phrase that a check misreads.** `tools/check-upstream-names.py` of `main` read "over `A` (`TauCeti.Algebra.WeilRestriction.instHopfAlgebra`, …)" as a declaration `A`. The row is rephrased so that `instHopfAlgebra` and `instSmooth` stand before their modules and are checked.

The cases that were checked and needed no change: the empty set of points does not occur (a point of the Ran space is a nonempty set); over the empty test scheme every disc is empty and every statement is trivial; a finite group has `L⁺H = H` in characteristic zero, which LG (2) and (3) allow by "when H has positive dimension"; over a disconnected test scheme the statements are natural, and the only place where it matters is the coproduct of formal discs (Codex 4).
