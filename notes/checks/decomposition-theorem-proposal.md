### 2.1 What the user needs

`Satake.icSheaf_restriction_isParity`, step (3), applies the theorem to one kind of map: the Bott–Samelson resolution `π : BS(w) → X_w` of the closure `X_w` of an I-orbit in `Gr_G`, for `G` simply connected and `w` of minimal length in its coset (`AffineGrassmannian.bottSamelsonResolution`).

- **The map.** Proper, between projective varieties over ℂ; the source is smooth and connected (an iterated `ℙ¹`-bundle); the map is an isomorphism over the open I-orbit.
- **The complex.** The constant sheaf of the source, shifted by its dimension.
- **The coefficients.** ℂ. The node on `main` does not say so; its user `Satake.ic_starRestriction_isEven` carries the result to D-modules through `DModules.analyticDeRham_isEquivalence`, whose target is `D^b_c(Y, ℂ)`. The node on the branch says "with coefficients C".
- **The conclusion used.** The IC sheaf of `X_w` is a direct summand of `π_*ℂ[d]`. This follows from two facts: `π_*ℂ[d]` is the sum of the shifts of its perverse cohomology sheaves, and the zeroth of them is semisimple. Then the summand that is not zero on the open orbit is the IC sheaf ([BBD] Théorème 4.3.1).
- **Not used.** The other summands, local systems on strata (the strata are simply connected), maps that are not birational, coefficients other than the constant sheaf, relative hard Lefschetz.

The plan's statement on `main` is: for a proper `f` from a smooth connected `Y`, `f_*ℂ_Y[dim Y]` is a finite sum of shifted intersection complexes of semisimple local systems on strata. This is more than the user needs (any proper map, all summands). It is also narrower than its source: [BBD] Théorème 6.2.5 is for every `K` that is semisimple of geometric origin. The proof of the theorem needs the wider class, because the class must be closed under the operations. So the form of the source is the natural generality ("Generality" in `STATUS.md`: a theorem that serves only a proof is stated under the hypotheses its proof uses, with the case used as an instance).

### 2.2 What `decompositionTheoremComplex` states

EtaleDualityAndPerverseSheaves, README §5.10 "Decomposition and relative Lefschetz over the complex numbers", paragraph "The decomposition theorem over ℂ":

> Let `f : X → Y` be a proper morphism of schemes of finite type over `ℂ`. If `K ∈ D^b_c(X(ℂ), ℂ)` is semisimple of geometric origin, then `Rf_*K` is semisimple of geometric origin: `Rf_*K ≅ ⊕_i pH^i(Rf_*K)[−i]` and each `pH^i(Rf_*K)` is a finite direct sum of simple perverse sheaves of geometric origin. The same holds with `ℂ` replaced by `ℚ̄_ℓ` in the étale topology. In particular, for `X` smooth (resp. any `X` and `L` of geometric origin), `Rf_*ℂ_X[dim X]` (resp. `Rf_*IC_X(L)`) decomposes in this way. No `ℚ`- or `ℚ_ℓ`-rational descent of the splitting is asserted.

- **Hypotheses.** `X` separated of finite type over ℂ (the scope of the roadmap is separated schemes of finite type); `f` proper; `K` semisimple of geometric origin; coefficients ℂ.
- **Geometric origin** (§5.9, [BBD] 6.2.4). The simple perverse sheaves of complex vector spaces of geometric origin are the smallest class that contains ℂ on the point and is closed under the simple constituents of `pH^i(T F)` for `T` one of `Rf_*`, `Rf_!`, `f^*`, `Rf^!`, and of `pH^i(A ⊗ B)` and `pH^i RHom(A, B)`. `K` is semisimple of geometric origin if `K ≅ ⊕ pH^i(K)[−i]` with each `pH^i(K)` a finite sum of such simple objects. The checks of §5.9 include: `ℂ_X[d]` is of geometric origin for `X` smooth connected of dimension `d`.
- **Needs.** §5.9, §5.6, §3.5. **Sources.** [BBD] Théorème 6.2.5 with its proof 6.2.7; de Cataldo–Migliorini 2009, Theorem 1.6.1.
- **Route.** Reduce to a simple `K` of geometric origin. Such a `K` is built from the constant sheaf on the point by finitely many operations. The comparison of étale and analytic sheaves (§3.5 `complexAnalyticComparison`), through a chosen isomorphism `ℂ ≅ ℚ̄_ℓ`, carries this construction to étale ℓ-adic sheaves. It does not carry an arbitrary analytic complex: for `E` coefficients its essential image is the complexes with monodromy-stable lattices. The restricted specialization of §5.9 (`pureSpecializationOfGeometricOrigin`) carries the object to one over the algebraic closure of a finite field that comes from a pure perverse sheaf over the finite field. Purity is a statement over the finite field (Weil II, DeligneWeightsAndPurity §§7.5–7.9). The splitting of the pushforward, with semisimple perverse cohomology, holds after extension to the algebraic closure of the finite field (§5.6 `decompositionTheorem`), and is carried back.
- **It covers the user.** The case of the user is the "In particular" clause for smooth `X`. Its input, the constant sheaf, has an integral lattice.

**The observation.** In `plans/`, `Satake.sphericalHeart_semisimple` has two users: `Satake.tannakaRootDatum_equiv_dual`, which uses it for finite type, connectedness and reductivity of the Tannakian group ([MV] §7), and `Satake.geometricSatakeHeartEquivalence`, which has it in its `deps` and builds its equivalence from `Satake.tannakaGroup` and `Satake.dualIdentification`. The texts of the nodes above them use the functor `Rep(Ǧ) → Sph^♥`; `Satake.naiveFactorizationFunctor` says "The statement uses only this functor".

**What is established.** Let `G̃` be the Tannakian group of the spherical heart `P` with convolution, fusion and the fibre functor (`Satake.tannakaGroup`, which uses no semisimplicity; the heart is rigid by `Satake.sphericalHeartDual`, which uses none either). The field is algebraically closed of characteristic 0. The checker agrees with steps 1 to 3 in the form below. [DeligneMilne] is in `references/` (the version of 11 February 2022); the three statements cited below were read in its text extraction.

1. Let `X` be the sum of the `IC_λ` for generators `λ` of the dominant monoid and their duals, and `⟨X⟩` the tensor subcategory of subquotients of sums of convolution powers of `X`. Its group `G_X`, a quotient of `G̃`, is of finite type and acts faithfully on the fibre of `X` ([DeligneMilne] Propositions 2.20(b) and 2.21(a)).
2. `G_X` is connected. `IC_{nλ}` is a constituent of the n-th convolution power of `IC_λ` (the support argument of [MV] §12), so no tensor subcategory other than that of the unit has finitely many simple objects ([DeligneMilne] Corollary 2.22). This step does not use semisimplicity. It must come before step 3: a finite group has a faithful semisimple representation and a trivial unipotent radical.
3. `X` is semisimple, so the faithful representation is semisimple. A normal unipotent subgroup fixes a nonzero vector in every nonzero representation, and its fixed vectors form a subrepresentation; so the unipotent radical acts trivially on every simple representation, hence on a faithful semisimple one, hence it is trivial. So `G_X` is connected and reductive, and all its representations are semisimple (`TauCeti.reductiveCommHopfAlgProperty_iff_linearlyReductive`, whose hypotheses are characteristic 0, finite type and geometric connectedness). So `⟨X⟩` is semisimple. It contains every `IC_λ`, so it is the full subcategory of the semisimple objects of `P`.
4. The weight functors give a homomorphism from the dual torus `Ť` to `G_X`; it is a maximal torus. The weights of the fibre of `IC_λ` lie in the convex hull of `Wλ`, and each `wλ` occurs once. So the Weyl group and the directions of the roots and coroots of `G_X` are those of the dual group. This part is the checker's derivation; I agree with it.

**What is open.**

- **The root datum.** [MV] §7 gets the weights of `L(λ)`, its statement (a2), from Proposition 3.10 on the standard sheaves `I_!(λ)` and `I_*(λ)` together with Lemma 7.1, which identifies them with `IC_λ`. Without Lemma 7.1 one has only a containment of weights, and each simple root of `G_X` is `α̌` or `2α̌`; the second is possible only when `α` is divisible by 2 in the character lattice. The route is that of [MV] §12, which is in this state for positive characteristic: the centre, from the grading by the components of `Gr_G`, gives the inclusions of the root lattices and settles adjoint `G`; then semisimple `G` through `G → G_ad`, and a general `G` through its derived group. One step of §12 is not available here: it uses that the group of `G_ad` is reductive, which §12 takes from the case of characteristic 0 by a count of dimensions. For the quotients `G_X` this step has to be redone, with the functors between the hearts of `Gr_G` and `Gr_{G_ad}`. The other way is a local computation: the weight functor of `λ − α̌` is not zero on `IC_λ` for one `λ`, which is a statement about a stratum of codimension 2. Neither is written in a paper of `references/`, and I have not estimated the cost.
- **The kernel of `G̃ → G_X`.** It is trivial exactly when the heart is semisimple. So the argument identifies `G_X`, not `G̃`. `Satake.dualIdentification` is an isomorphism from "the Tannakian group" to the dual group, and `Satake.tannakaPinning` is a pinning of "the Tannakian group of the spherical heart", which "is constant over X and over X^I". Both are about the group of the whole heart. With the argument above they would be statements about `G_X`, the group of the semisimple objects: a different object until semisimplicity is proved. Its constancy over `X` and `X^I` ([MV] §5) would have to be shown for the semisimple objects in families.
