# The universal property of the enhanced derived category: a clause of the node, or a node of its own

Branch `proposal/enhanced-derived-universal-property`, started on `main` at `df74883` and merged with `main` at `e0d5ff1`. This note is steps 1 and 2 of `prompts/change.md`. Rounds 1 and 2 of the checks changed the points that `enhanced-derived-universal-property-answers-1.md` and `enhanced-derived-universal-property-answers-2.md` list, and this note states them as corrected. Section 1 is the problem, section 2 the sources, section 3 the users of the universal property, section 4 the options, section 5 the choice, section 6 the change on the branch, section 7 the counts, and section 8 the points that stay open.

## 1. The problem

The roadmap node `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategory` (`plans/Roadmap/EnhancedDerivedSheaves/EnhancedDerived.toml`) stated four things for a Grothendieck abelian category `A`: the quasicategory `D∞(A)` with its functor `Q` from the nerve of the complexes; its stability; the triangulated equivalence of its homotopy category with `DerivedCategory A`; and the universal property of `Q` as a localization of quasicategories at the quasi-isomorphisms (a Dwyer–Kan localization). Tau Ceti at the pin `ea7f36aa9d31d22b2b362a282632d3956a8d22de` has the quasicategory and `Q` (`TauCeti.KInjectiveDGCategory.fromNerve`, with the detection of quasi-isomorphisms and essential surjectivity). It has no localization of quasicategories, and the module doc comment of `TauCeti/CategoryTheory/DG/CochainComplex/Localization.lean` says: "The universal property of `TauCeti.KInjectiveDGCategory.fromNerve` as a localization of quasicategories at the quasi-isomorphisms is not proved here."

The 18 placeholders of `GLCinLean/Statement/Enhanced.lean` all rest on `Statement.DGCatCarrier`. On `main`, nine nodes are below it: `Infinity.linearCategory`, `Infinity.derivedVect`, `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategory`, `Infinity.homotopyCategoryPretriangulated`, `Infinity.presentableCategory`, `Infinity.monoidalCategory`, `Infinity.moduleCategory`, `Infinity.limits` and `Infinity.mappingSpaces`. `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategory` is reached only through `Infinity.derivedVect`. A placeholder is replaced only when every node below it is proved (`AGENTS.md`, "The statement"). So the placeholders wait for the universal property, a result that the search of the pin rates as large.

The question: does `Infinity.derivedVect`, `Infinity.linearCategory` or the statement use the universal property? If not, should it be a node that only its users depend on?

## 3. Who uses the universal property

How the roadmap states it. E1.3 names five parts of its target: "*enhancedDerived* (the equivalence with the supplied enhancement), *enhancedDerived.homotopyCategory* …, *enhancedDerived.mappingSpaces* …, *enhancedDerived.isStable* … and *enhancedDerived.detection*" (line 525). The universal property is one sentence of the paragraph on *enhancedDerived* (line 523): "More precisely, the functor from the nerve of complexes to `D∞(A)` is a Dwyer–Kan localization at the quasi-isomorphisms (the supplied localization universal property; HA Proposition 1.3.5.15)." It belongs to the part *enhancedDerived* and to none of the four dotted parts. The paragraph cites "HA Definition 1.3.5.8 and Propositions 1.3.5.9 and 1.3.5.15": the object is a definition, the dg nerve of the K-injective complexes, and the universal property is a proposition about it. The notion of a localization comes from another roadmap: E0.3 takes "relative localization with its full functor-space universal property" from G2.11, which says "Construct relative localization (C,W) … Prove its functor-space universal property". Tau Ceti lands E1.3 in parts already: `fromNerve` and the detection are at the pin, without the universal property.

What the papers ask of `Vect`. [AGKRRV] §0.9.3: "We will denote by $\Vect_\sfe$ the stable $\infty$-category of chain complexes of $\sfe$-modules", "equipped with a symmetric monoidal structure". §0.9.4 defines `DGCat` as the presentable stable ∞-categories "equipped with a module structure over $\Vect_\sfe$". [IndCoh] §0.6.4: "stable $\infty$-categories tensored over $\Vect$, where the latter is the DG category of complexes of $k$-vector spaces". None of them states a universal property of `Vect`. So the statements of the nodes below `DGCatCarrier` do not use the theorem. The construction of one of them does.

## 4. Options

- **A. No change.** The universal property stays a clause of `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategory`, and every user of the node waits for it.
- **B. A node of its own in `plans/`.** The theorem is a roadmap node above `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategory`; its three users depend on it.
- **C. Out of `plans/`.** The theorem moves to `plans-proof/` or is dropped.

## 5. The choice: B, with no saving for the placeholders

- **Against C.** Three nodes of `plans/` use the theorem. Dropping a theorem that the plan asks for would also weaken the plan.
- **For B over A.** The decision "Faster within Tau Ceti's rules" (`STATUS.md`) keeps a dependency between two roadmap nodes "only when the statement or the definition of the upper node needs the lower one". Under option A every user of the node waits for the theorem. Under option B the dependencies say which node uses it: `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategoryTStructure` uses neither the theorem nor a node that does, and no longer waits for it; `Sheaves.schemeQCoh` and `Constructible.DerivedCategory` do not use it themselves and still wait, through `Infinity.derivedVect` and `Infinity.derivedBimoduleTransfer`. The second reason is the two results of G2.11 that the plan needs, the localization of a quasicategory and the localization of a symmetric monoidal category. They belong to another roadmap area, so they are nodes of that area, or `lake exe tracker` does not see the wait for them (`STATUS.md`, "Roadmap targets without a plan node"). With `Infinity.IsLocalization` as a node, the theorem is the only part of the old node that uses it, and a clause inside the node would make every user of the enhanced derived category wait for a notion of G2.11. The third reason: after the split each roadmap node is the statement of its target items, so `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategory` can be folded in as "Plan" of `AGENTS.md` says when its items `enhanced-derived-stable` and `enhanced-derived-category` are at the pin, without the large theorem. Option B changes no definition and weakens none: the object of `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategory` is the same declaration of Tau Ceti, `TauCeti.dgNerve R (TauCeti.KInjectiveDGCategory A)`, with the same functor, stability and comparison with `DerivedCategory A`, and the theorem stays in the plan with the same target items. The rule "give each definition … its universal property where it has one" still holds for the roadmap: the theorem is a node directly above the definition and a target item of the Worker.
- **The split follows the roadmap.** The theorem is a sentence of E1.3 with its own reference (HA Proposition 1.3.5.15). The notion of a localization belongs to another roadmap (G2.11). `STATUS.md` records a precedent: the t-structure of the same node is `Infinity.enhancedDerivedCategoryTStructure`.
- **`Infinity.derivedVect` keeps its wait, and why.** Its symmetric monoidal structure needs a construction with all coherences, in the form of the pin: a coCartesian fibration over the nerve of `FinPointed` with the Segal condition. Three candidates were examined, and none gives a shorter route. This does not show that every construction needs the theorem: it shows that no shorter route with a source was found.
  - The dg nerve of a "dg category of operators". There is none: with products of the morphism complexes the composition is multilinear in the components of a map, and with tensor products the fibre over ⟨2⟩ is the dg nerve of a tensor product, which is not the product of two dg nerves.
  - Products of dg nerves glued by a comparison map from `N_dg(C) × N_dg(D)` to `N_dg(C ⊗ D)`. A natural such map is not both associative and symmetric: on 2-simplices it has one coefficient `α`, associativity forces `α² = α` and symmetry forces `2α = 1`.
  - The symmetric monoidal structure of the dg nerve in the literature (Drew, Theorem 2.4, after Hinich, §3). It is a statement about functors of quasicategories, and it is built by Dwyer–Kan localization of the categories of dg categories and of categories enriched in simplicial modules. Hinich notes that the Dold–Kan functor behind the mapping spaces is lax monoidal and "not colax symmetric monoidal", and he identifies the result for the complexes with a symmetric monoidal localization, using that the dg nerve is the localization. These localizations are other theorems than the localization of the complexes, they are not at the pin, and they are larger than the roadmap's route; the comparison with the localized complexes (Hinich §3.2.4) is a step after the construction.
  
  So the plan takes the roadmap's route: the nerve of the category of operators of the complexes, its localization at the quasi-isomorphisms, and the theorem to identify the result with the dg nerve. `Infinity.derivedVect` depends on the theorem and on the localization of a symmetric monoidal category.

They are not changed here ("Correct the nodes needed for the current work"); `STATUS.md` has an open item.
5. **`Infinity.derivedBimoduleTransfer`.** Its dependency on the theorem rests on the roadmap's proof route for the tensor product over a sheaf of commutative rings. Its direct image (2) may not need the theorem.
