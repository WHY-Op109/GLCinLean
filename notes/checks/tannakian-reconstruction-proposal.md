## Summary

- **Chosen option.** The reconstruction of the group of a neutral Tannakian category, and the three criteria that the plan uses (finite type, connectedness, closed immersion), become four roadmap nodes of MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles §§6.1–6.3. What is written here is the proof that the spherical heart satisfies the hypotheses.

## 1. The problem

The node `Satake.tannakaGroup` (module `GLCinLean.GeometricLanglands.Satake.Reconstruction`, source [MV] Proposition 11.1, provider “here”) plans three things: a Hopf algebra built from the dual endomorphism coalgebras of projective generators and from fusion; the equivalence of the spherical heart with its finite comodules; and the identification of its points with the tensor automorphisms of the fibre functor, with `TauCeti.Tannaka.pointsFunctorIsoTensorAutFunctor`. It depends on `Satake.weightProjectives` and `Satake.cohomologyFiberFunctor`. Two nodes of `plans/` use it: `Satake.tannakaRootDatum_equiv_dual` and `Satake.geometricSatakeHeartEquivalence`. One node of `plans-proof/` uses it: `SpectralProof.geometricCenter_operMap`.

The first two things are the theorem of Deligne and Milne on neutral Tannakian categories ([DeligneMilne] Theorem 2.11) in one special case. That theorem is a target of an accepted Tau Ceti roadmap. `AGENTS.md`, “Sources of code”, says that such a part goes to Tau Ceti and that a second copy here would compete with it. `Satake.tannakaRootDatum_equiv_dual` also calls the criteria of [DeligneMilne] Proposition 2.20 and Corollary 2.22 “explicit obligations here”; the same roadmap states them. This is audit finding B6 (`notes/audit-2026-11-18.md`, confirmed by `notes/audit-2026-11-19.md`).

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 What MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles §§6.1–6.3 state

The roadmap is `TauCetiRoadmap/MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles/README.md` (TauCetiRoadmap #1313, merged 2026-11-17, unchanged at `fe56342a`). Its “Scope and ownership” says that it owns “neutral and relative Tannaka reconstruction”, and that “reconstruction of a *given* affine group from its representations belong[s] to **ReductiveGroups**, Layers 0–1, and the corresponding Tau Ceti modules”.

- **The definition (§6.1).** “A neutral fiber datum over a field `K` consists of an essentially small `K`-linear abelian rigid symmetric monoidal category `C`, an identification `End(1)=K`, and a `K`-linear exact faithful strong symmetric monoidal functor `ω:C→Vect_fd(K)` with ordinary symmetry. It contains no group or Hopf algebra.” The field is any field. Rigidity is a hypothesis. The symmetry of the target is the ordinary one; a check of §6.1 says that super vector spaces with the Koszul symmetry “in characteristic different from two do not have an ordinary symmetric forgetful fiber” (in characteristic 2 the Koszul sign is the ordinary symmetry). §3.6 defines Tannakian and neutral Tannakian categories as terminology, for a field of characteristic 0.
- **The form of the group (§6.2).** A commutative Hopf algebra `B`: the filtered coalgebra of the duals of the finite endomorphism algebras `A_X ⊂ End_K(ωX)` of §6.1, with a commutative bialgebra structure from the tensor product and an antipode from the duals of `C`. “The resulting affine group scheme `G_ω=Spec B` represents `Aut^⊗(ω)` and gives a symmetric monoidal equivalence `C≃Rep_fd(G_ω)`.” The representations are the finite right comodules of Tau Ceti (`TauCeti.FGComoduleCat`). The functor of points is on all commutative `K`-algebras (“Reconstruct natural tensor endomorphisms on every value algebra `R` as algebra maps `B→R`”).
- **The signature.** `Suggested.lean` states `neutralTannakian_recognition`: for `K : Type u` a field, `C : Type v` with `Category.{u} C`, `EssentiallySmall.{u} C`, `Abelian C`, `Linear K C`, `MonoidalCategory C`, `MonoidalPreadditive C`, `MonoidalLinear K C`, `SymmetricCategory C`, `RigidCategory C`, a bijective map `c ↦ c • 𝟙 (𝟙_ C)`, and `ω : C ⥤ FGModuleCat K` with `ω.Braided`, `ω.Additive`, `ω.Linear K`, `ω.Faithful`, `PreservesFiniteLimits ω`, `PreservesFiniteColimits ω`: there exist a commutative Hopf algebra `H : Type u`, an equivalence `E : C ≌ TauCeti.FGComoduleCat.{u, u, u} K H` with `E.functor.Braided`, and a monoidal isomorphism, in `SemimoduleCat K`, between `E.functor ⋙ forget₂ _ (SemimoduleCat K)` and `ω ⋙ forget₂ _ (ModuleCat K)` followed by `ModuleCat.equivalenceSemimoduleCat`. The Hopf algebra is under an existential quantifier. The comparisons of fibre functors in these sections have three codomains. `coalgebra_reconstruction` of §5.2 compares `E.functor ⋙ forget₂ _ (ModuleCat K)` with `ω ⋙ forget₂ _ (ModuleCat K)`, in `ModuleCat K` and with no monoidal condition. The recognition of §6.2 compares in `SemimoduleCat K`, after both forgetful composites. The marking `ω_C ∘ U ≅ ω_D` of §6.3 has no signature; it is between two fibre functors, whose target is `FGModuleCat K`. The header of the file says that it “is not the roadmap and is not exhaustive”; the README is the definitive text.
- **The criteria (§6.3).** Finite: “`G_ω` is finite precisely when one object generates `C` under finite sums and subquotients.” Finite type: “precisely when one object tensor-generates using its dual and then finite sums and subquotients” ([DeligneMilne] Proposition 2.20). Connectedness: “In characteristic zero, prove the connectedness criterion using nontrivial finite tensor subcategories: for every object with nontrivial action, its finite sum/subquotient hull cannot already be tensor closed. State the characteristic-zero restriction and the meaning of nontrivial action explicitly” (Corollary 2.22). Homomorphisms: “For a tensor functor `U:D→C` with a marked fiber comparison, construct `G_C→G_D`. Prove it faithfully flat exactly when `U` is fully faithful and its image is closed under ambient subobjects; it is a closed immersion exactly when every object of `C` is a subquotient of an object in the image” (Proposition 2.21). These have no signature in `Suggested.lean`; they are targets by the text of the section.
- **What the sections need.** The neutral arguments of §§6.1–6.3 do not depend on the geometric layers. The “Dependencies” of Layer 3 say: “Abstract neutral and relative reconstruction is in Layer 6 and can be proved independently of the geometric layers.” Those of Layer 5 say: “The neutral abstract arguments of Layer 6 are independent of this construction and may be proved first”. The “Dependencies” of Layer 6 name Mathlib and “Tau Ceti's finite-comodule, rigidity and existing known-group representation API”, and then say: “Layer 5 supplies the diagram category and geometric comparison; §6.8 supplies the second fiber extension.” So the Nori applications in Layer 6 use Layer 5; the neutral and relative reconstruction arguments do not. §5.2 of the roadmap states `coalgebra_reconstruction` (signature in `Suggested.lean`): an essentially small abelian `K`-linear category with an exact faithful additive linear functor to `FGModuleCat K` is equivalent to the finite comodules of a coalgebra, over the fibre functor (the comparison is in `ModuleCat K`). It needs no tensor structure and no rigidity, and it is the coalgebra step of §6.2; one proof at that generality serves both.
- **Not stated.** [DeligneMilne] Proposition 1.20 (a tensor category with a fibre functor is rigid when every object with a fibre of dimension 1 has an inverse for the tensor product). No roadmap README that cites Deligne–Milne cites it. The first half of its proof is in the roadmap: §6.2 gives the coalgebra its commutative bialgebra structure before it uses the duals, and §6.4 does the same under its relative hypotheses (a check there: “Finite representations of the matrix monoid test bialgebra reconstruction without rigidity; no antipode is concluded”). The second half, from the invertibility of the objects of rank one to an antipode, is in no roadmap.

### 2.4 What [MV] does in characteristic 0, and the weight projectives

[MV] has two constructions of the group.

- **§7, “The case of a field of characteristic zero”.** “Here we make use of Tannakian formalism, using [DM] as a general reference. In section 11, where we work over an arbitrary base ring `k`, we carry out the constructions explicitly without referring to the general Tannakian formalism.” The section verifies “the conditions of the proposition 1.20 in [DM] and then also the conditions of the theorem 2.11 in [DM]”, concludes that a group scheme exists, and identifies it (Theorem 7.3). It uses no projective object.
- **§§9–11.** §9 (Proposition 9.1, Corollary 9.2) represents the weight functors on a closed finite union `Z` of orbits by projectives; §10 (Proposition 10.1) shows that `F(P_Z(k))` is free over `k` and commutes with change of `k`; §11 (Proposition 11.1) builds from them the coalgebra as the filtered colimit `B(k) = colim_Z B_Z(k)`, and says: “Unlike [DM] we work over an arbitrary commutative ring `k`. This is made possible by the fact that `F(P_Z(k))` is free over `k`.” The introduction of §6 says the same of the tensor structure: “the results of this present section are used in section 7 only in the case when `k` is a field”.

So in the main line of [MV] the projectives are not used over a field of characteristic 0; they are the route for its other coefficient rings (the standing assumption of [MV] §2 is a commutative Noetherian ring of finite global dimension, and “with suitable modifications” any commutative ring). One remark of [MV] is an exception. Remark 7.2 says that Lemma 7.1 (semisimplicity) can be avoided: “One must then ignore this section and first go through the rest of the paper in the case when `k` is a field of characteristic zero. The arguments of section 12, in a greatly simplified form, then give theorem 7.3.” That route goes through §§8–12, where the projectives are used. The plan does not take it: `Satake.tannakaRootDatum_equiv_dual` says that Remark 7.2 “is not used”. The plan's setting is an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0.

### 2.5 What each user needs from `Satake.tannakaGroup`

This is the torsor of the tensor isomorphisms from the standard fibre functor, extended to the centre, to that functor ([DeligneMilne] Theorem 3.2; roadmap §6.8). `Satake.tannakaGroup` gives the tensor automorphisms of one fibre functor and not this torsor. ### 2.6 Rigidity

The roadmap's theorem has `RigidCategory C` as a hypothesis. For a field of characteristic 0, [MV] §7 gets rigidity from [DeligneMilne] Proposition 1.20. The plan cites that proposition in its corrected form (the version of 2022, footnote 5: the object `U` of its conditions (d) and (e) must be an identity object; as first printed it was false). The proof that the paper indicates ([DeligneMilne] Remark 2.18) goes through the affine monoid scheme of the tensor endomorphisms of the fibre functor and a determinant argument. The roadmap builds that bialgebra (§6.2, and §6.4 under relative hypotheses) and does not state the proposition. [MV] §11 names the dual directly. On the objects whose total cohomology is projective over the coefficient ring, it defines the anti-involution `A ↦ 𝔻(i^*A)` and says, without proof, that it makes the representations of the group scheme on finitely generated projective modules “a rigid tensor category”. Over a field these are all objects of the heart; over ℤ they are not (for the trivial group the object ℤ/2 has no dual). The branch records this gap in the root file `GLCinLean.lean`. The accepted roadmap GeometricSatakeAndFusion has the same statement for its own category (§2.10 “Duals of Satake objects”: “The right dual is `sw*D(A)`; evaluation and coevaluation come from the adjunction of proper relative ULA kernels and satisfy the two triangle identities … No symmetry or fibre-functor monoidality is assumed”). On `main`, rigidity is only the words “prove the required tensor and rigidity constraints” in `Satake.cohomologyFiberFunctor`. The geometric dual is chosen for three reasons: it needs nothing that the roadmap does not state, it uses no fibre functor and no commutativity, and it gives the dual object by a formula.

### 2.7 Does another accepted roadmap own a part of what stays here?

GeometricSatakeAndFusion (#1235) is the only accepted roadmap on geometric Satake. Its “Conventions” and §4.7 fix its setting: sheaves with prime-to-`p` torsion or `ℓ`-adic coefficients, `ℓ ≠ p`, on the spaces of modifications over the relative Fargues–Fontaine curve of a nonarchimedean local field, and of `ℚ̄_ℓ`-sheaves on the Witt-vector affine Grassmannian over an algebraic closure of a finite field (its §4.7). The plan's category is that of D-modules on the affine Grassmannian over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. No comparison between the two exists in a roadmap, so none of its Satake targets (§2.10 duals, §3.4 fusion, §4.6 reductivity, §4.11 root datum) can serve the plan. What it shows is the division of the work: the general theorems from MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles, the hypotheses for the Satake category from the Satake roadmap.

### 2.8 Does an accepted roadmap need a part that stays here?

The parts that stay here are statements about the spherical heart of D-modules: its fibre functor, its duals, its Tannakian group, and the root datum of that group. No accepted roadmap has this category, so none needs them.

Two steps inside `Satake.tannakaRootDatum_equiv_dual` are general, and each has a provider already: the criteria for finite type and connectedness and the homomorphism from a grading (now roadmap nodes); and the passage from complete reducibility to reductivity (`TauCeti.reductiveCommHopfAlgProperty_iff_linearlyReductive`, at the pin). The isomorphism theorem (`Groups.splitReductiveGroup`, roadmap ReductiveGroups) is used in `Satake.dualIdentification`, to lift the identification of the based root data to an isomorphism of pinned groups. The last step of that node, reading the root datum from the weights of the irreducible representations, is a statement about a split reductive group with a maximal torus in characteristic 0. GeometricSatakeAndFusion §4.11 reaches the root datum by another route (constant terms for minimal Levi subgroups), so it does not need this step. When the work reaches the node, the writer states this step as a lemma on reductive groups in its canonical home; it is not changed here.

## 3. The options

**(a) Roadmap nodes for the reconstruction and its criteria; nodes here for the spherical heart.**

- Cost: four roadmap nodes; one new node here for rigidity; one author worker on MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles §§6.1–6.3, or a place in the queue of an existing one (the session decides). The work in Tau Ceti is large (the finite hulls, the coalgebra, the bialgebra, the antipode), but it is general, it has five other users, and it does not depend on the geometric layers.
- Risk: the Satake line waits for Tau Ceti's review. It waits in any case: the geometric inputs to `Satake.tannakaGroup` wait for D-modules and loop groups. The abstract reconstruction module is ready independently. A second risk is the form of the target: the headline is an existence statement, and the plan needs the Hopf algebra as data. The README states the group as a construction, the uniqueness follows from `pointsFunctorIsoTensorAutFunctor`, and the node and the worker's guidance ask for the definition.
