## 1. Approve the Setting refactor at `ecbb930`

I read the proposal with `git show` and ran Lean on temporary copies of the statement file from both revisions. The elaborated `Setting.mk` loses exactly two parameters. Its curve, morphism to `Spec k`, smoothness of relative dimension one, properness, connectedness, pinned group, square root with its squaring isomorphism, and identified dual group all have the same types as before over an admissible field. In particular, `RootDatumDuality` takes only `[Field k]`. The record asks for chosen data, so forming its type requires no theorem asserting that such choices exist over every field.

I checked the signatures of all eight placeholders, `IsLanglandsFunctor`, its iff theorem and the main theorem. Each still takes `[Field k] [CharZero k] [IsAlgClosed k]`. The main theorem still quantifies over every setting over such a field, asserts existence of a functor with both characterizing properties, and asserts equivalence for every such functor. The body defining the two properties is untouched. The broader record records ordinary connectedness of the curve over an arbitrary field. The theorem continues to use it over an algebraically closed field. Both copies elaborated with only the main theorem's existing `sorry` warning.

**Recommended change.** Accept Claude's recommendation and merge `proposal/setting-instances` as reviewed, after owner approval. Mark the record refactor done in the open item and supersede decision 5 of 2026-11-13. Audit each placeholder's two explicit hypotheses when replacing it. Retain the planned positive-rank example `Examples.exists_setting_rank_pos` as a witness when replacing the opaque duality data. Follow the merge checks and branch cleanup already specified in `STATUS.md`.

## 2. Keep every finite-type affine algebraic group H

I agree with Claude's option **(b)**. Both `Ran.Representations.equivCollisionLimit` and `Ran.Representations.linearFunctorEquivComodule` supply equivalences that downstream definitions use as data. The present applications to Ǧ and the trivial group explain why the results are needed. Raskin gives the construction for an affine algebraic group Γ in characteristic zero. In the geometric comparison, the Čech terms **and their limit** Rep(H) must be dualizable as DG categories. For the comodule equivalence, §6.15 uses compact generation, a compact unit and tensor duals for the compact objects. Neither argument needs reductivity. [Raskin I, §§6.15–6.23 and Lemma 6.32.1](https://www.samraskin.net/cpsi.pdf#page=43) · [Raskin II, §9.1, Lemma 9.8.1 and Appendix A.5](https://www.samraskin.net/cpsii.pdf#page=95)

The justification in `STATUS.md` needs a correction. [DG2] explicitly leaves compact generation of QCoh for general QCA stacks open. For **BH** the following proof supplies both inputs.

1. BH is QCA. The atlas by a point gives quasi-compactness, the diagonal is affine, and the stabilizers are forms of the finite-type group H. Thus `Sheaves.qcaGlobalSections_continuous`, already in `plans/`, gives a compact unit. A finite-dimensional rational representation V is a vector bundle on BH with tensor dual Vᵛ. Mapping out of V is computed by global sections after tensoring with Vᵛ, so V is compact. [[DG2], Thm. 1.4.2 and Cor. 1.4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5351v3#page=22)
2. Apply [DG2] Lemma 2.6.3 to the linear action of H on a point. It gives generation of QCoh(BH) by its heart. Each object of the heart is a rational comodule and is the filtered union of its finite-dimensional subcomodules. At the pin, `TauCeti.Subcomodule.exists_finite_subcomodule_mem` supplies the elementwise assertion, and finite sums give directedness. Hence finite-dimensional representations compactly generate. Their thick closure consists of tensor-dualizable objects and contains all compacts. This gives §6.15 rigidity and the sufficient criterion in the roadmap node `Infinity.rigidMonoidalCategory`. Hall and Rydh also give compact generation by irreducible representations for precisely this class of groups. [[DG2], Lemma 2.6.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5351v3#page=29) · [Hall–Rydh, Thm. A(c)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.1890#page=1)

**Recommended change.** In `Group/Representations.toml`, replace the reductive-only sentence with “For every affine group scheme H of finite type over a field of characteristic zero, the finite-dimensional rational representations are compact generators of Rep(H). Its unit is compact, every compact object has a tensor dual, and Rep(H) is rigid.” Record the proof above, add `Sheaves.qcaGlobalSections_continuous` to `deps`, and add [DG2] Thm. 1.4.2, Lemma 2.6.3 and §6.4 to `source`. Keep the underlying definition Rep(H) = QCoh(BH). In `Ran/RepresentationDescent.toml` and `Ran/RegularComodule.toml`, quantify over finite-type affine H in the project's characteristic-zero setting and nonempty finite I. Use the result about Rep(H) to discharge the hypotheses, including dualizability of the limit in (A.5.2). This proof uses the existing statement-plan QCA theorem directly.

## 3. Add four of the six obligations to `plans/`

I agree with Claude on the collision category, ULA, tensor-limit interchange, self-duality and comonadicity. I would also give the regular object its own node. The existing description of `equivCollisionLimit` promises to identify the forgetful functor and regular object. The later `linearFunctorEquivComodule` depends on that comparison and evaluates F on the same object. Putting the definition of the regular object inside the later node would force the earlier node to reach forward or describe the object twice.

**ULA ownership.** I checked the accepted EnhancedDerivedSheaves roadmap on `main` at `ca7079b6`, including its section contracts and `Suggested.lean`. E3.2–E3.3 cover adjoints and the compact-generator criterion for continuity. E5 covers scalar extension, compact generation, tensor products, dualizability, rigidity and Barr–Beck–Lurie. There is no target for Raskin's ULA objects or ULA generation. His object criterion tests compactness of Oblv(M) in QCoh(S) ⊗_{D(S)} C. His category criterion asks that the objects P ⊗ Oblv(M), for P perfect and M ULA, compactly generate that scalar extension. These depend on the specific forgetful functor and have the collision categories as a nontrivial application. [Accepted roadmap](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/ca7079b65a8a024fa92dc6d01724a402d53a23e0/TauCetiRoadmap/EnhancedDerivedSheaves/README.md) · [Raskin I, Appendix B](https://www.samraskin.net/cpsi.pdf#page=68)

Put those two definitions and their criteria in the local D-module library. For a ULA source, a continuous D(S)-linear functor admits a continuous linear right adjoint exactly when it preserves ULA objects (B.4.4 and B.7.1). Such a ULA-preserving functor is an equivalence if its restrictions to a closed subscheme and its open complement are equivalences (B.8.1). For the continuity step of B.7.1, move the existing roadmap entry `Infinity.rightAdjoint_continuous` from `plans-proof/` to `plans/` with its provider intact. Put the collision-category application under Ran.

In `equivCollisionLimit`, name the collision, ULA and regular-object nodes. In `linearFunctorEquivComodule`, name the comparison, the tensor-limit node with its self-duality corollary, and the regular-object node. This order gives the statement every construction it uses and leaves the lax-monoidal strengthening in the proof plan.
