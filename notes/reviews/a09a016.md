## Item 4: the second property against [GLC1] Cor. 1.6.5

Write `R = Rep(Ǧ)_Ran`, `Q = QCoh(LS_Ǧ)`, `D = Dmod_½(Bun_G)`, `P` for the vacuum Poincaré object, `H(V) = V ⋆ P`, and `T = Ψ ∘ F`. Property 2′ is the Lean form: an equivalence `Maps_D(H V, M) ≃ Maps_Q(Loc V, T M)` of functors `Rᵒᵖ × D → Spc`. Property 2 is the paper's form: `T ≃ 𝕃_{G,coarse}`.

**Facts from the paper that the comparison uses.**

- (a) [GLC1] Thm. 1.2.4 (lines 1459–1462) with §1.2.5 (lines 1466–1467): the action of `R` on `D` factors through `Loc`, and this defines the action of `Q` on `D`. So the action of `R` is the action of `Q` restricted along `Loc`, and `H ≃ B ∘ Loc` as functors `R → D`, where `B(E) = E ⋆ P`.
- (b) [GLC1] §§1.4.1, 1.4.2, 1.4.5 (lines 1684–1699, 1721–1723): `B` is the functor called `𝕃^L_{G,temp}`, and `𝕃_{G,coarse}` is its right adjoint.
- (c) [GLC1] §1.2.2 (lines 1451–1452): the right adjoint `Loc^R` of `Loc` is fully faithful. This is [GaitsgoryOutline] Prop. 4.3.4.

**Step A. Property 2 gives property 2′.** Uses (a) and (b) only. `Maps_Q(Loc V, T M) ≃ Maps_Q(Loc V, 𝕃_{G,coarse} M)` by property 2; `≃ Maps_D(B(Loc V), M)` by the adjunction (b); `≃ Maps_D(H V, M)` by (a). Each step is natural in `(V, M)` together, so the composite is one equivalence of bifunctors. Full faithfulness of `Loc^R` is not used. Compactness of `P` and rigidity of `Q` are not used.

**Step B. Property 2′ gives property 2.** Uses (a), (b) and (c). By (a) and (b), `Maps_D(H V, M) ≃ Maps_Q(Loc V, 𝕃_{G,coarse} M)`, naturally in `(V, M)`. With property 2′ this gives `Maps_Q(Loc V, T M) ≃ Maps_Q(Loc V, 𝕃_{G,coarse} M)`, so `Maps_R(V, Loc^R T M) ≃ Maps_R(V, Loc^R 𝕃_{G,coarse} M)`, naturally in `(V, M)`. Here `Loc^R` only has to exist as a functor of ∞-categories, which holds because `Loc` preserves colimits; its continuity is not used. By the Yoneda lemma in `V`, `Loc^R ∘ T ≃ Loc^R ∘ 𝕃_{G,coarse}` as functors `D → R`. This step needs naturality in `V` and `M` together. By (c), composition with `Loc^R` is fully faithful on functor categories, so `T ≃ 𝕃_{G,coarse}` as functors `D → Q`.

**What kind of equivalence each side carries.**

- Property 2′ is an equivalence of space-valued bifunctors. It carries the naturality of functors of ∞-categories and nothing `k`-linear.
- Step B therefore gives `Ψ ∘ F ≃ 𝕃_{G,coarse}` as functors of ∞-categories (in `Fun(D, Q)`). It does not give a `k`-linear natural equivalence (a 2-isomorphism of `DGCat`).
- Step A needs only an equivalence of functors of ∞-categories as input. A `k`-linear one gives one.

**What is equal.** Given (a), (b), (c): property 2′ holds for `F` if and only if `Ψ ∘ F ≃ 𝕃_{G,coarse}` as functors of ∞-categories. This is exactly the docstring's sentence.

**What is not shown equal.** If [GLC1] means `≃` between two 1-morphisms of `DGCat` as a `k`-linear equivalence, then property 2 implies property 2′, and the converse is not given by this argument. A `k`-linear functor can in general have more than one `k`-linear structure on the same functor of ∞-categories, so I do not see a formal converse. [GLC1] does not say which reading it means. This is U2.

**Step C. The main theorem is the same under both readings.** Let `F` be a continuous functor with property 1 and `Ψ ∘ F ≃ 𝕃_{G,coarse}` as functors of ∞-categories. On compact objects `F` lands in `IndCoh_Nilp(LS_Ǧ)^{>-∞}`, and `Ψ` is an equivalence `IndCoh_Nilp(LS_Ǧ)^{>-∞} → Q^{>-∞}` ([GLC1] §1.6.3, lines 1824–1826, from [ArinkinGaitsgory] Prop. 4.4.5). So the restriction of `F` to compact objects is fixed up to natural equivalence. `D` is compactly generated ([GLC1] §1.6.4, lines 1830–1831) and `F` is continuous, so `F` is fixed up to natural equivalence of functors of ∞-categories. This is the paper's own uniqueness argument, and it needs no `k`-linearity. So every functor with the Lean properties is naturally equivalent to the paper's `𝕃_G`, and is an equivalence exactly when `𝕃_G` is. The paper's `𝕃_G` has the Lean properties by Step A. Hence: the Lean existence half holds exactly when the paper's does, and the Lean "for all" half holds exactly when Conj. 1.6.7 does. The main theorem is equivalent to "the existence part of Cor. 1.6.5, and Conj. 1.6.7".

**Conditions of this equivalence.** It uses [GLC1] Thm. 1.2.4, the full faithfulness of `Loc^R`, [GLC1] Thm. 1.6.2, [ArinkinGaitsgory] Prop. 4.4.5 and compact generation of `D`. The last three are what the paper uses for Cor. 1.6.5 itself. Thm. 1.2.4 is cited in [GLC1], not proved there; [GaitsgoryOutline] Cor. 4.5.5 derives it from Prop. 4.3.4 and Thm. 4.5.2, whose proof that paper only sketches.

**The other docstring claims.** "This form uses neither the action of `QCoh(LS_Ǧ)` nor `𝕃_{G,coarse}`": correct, the Lean term names only `heckeActionRight`, `vacuumPoincare`, `Loc`, `Psi` and `F`. The module docstring's "in a form that agrees with the paper's by [GLC1] Thm. 1.2.4": correct, with U2.

## Hack-answer test

**State now.** `TStructuredDGCat`, `DGFun`, `DGFun.IsEquivalence` and `IsLanglandsFunctor` are definitions, but everything under them is `opaque`. Lean can prove only the four `Nonempty` instances, the two `Iff.rfl` lemmas and pure logic. The blind reader gave these models of the constants:

- trivially true: every carrier is one point, `DGNatIso` and `MapsEquiv` are one point, `IsEventuallyCoconnective` is always true;
- empty: `RootDatumDuality` is empty, so there is no `Setting`;
- false: `DGNatIso` is empty; or `MapsEquiv` is empty; or `IsCompact` is always true and `IsEventuallyCoconnective` always false.

So no example can rule out a degenerate case yet, and none is given. This agrees with `STATUS.md` line 8.

**The shape of the theorem resists the usual hacks.** A choice that makes the class of functors with both properties empty makes the existence half false. A choice that makes every functor an equivalence leaves the existence half, which is a real theorem of the paper. A degenerate object in the second property (`Rep(Ǧ)_Ran` zero, `Loc` zero, or the Poincaré object zero) makes the theorem false, not easy: with the Poincaré object zero, property 2′ forces `Ψ ∘ F = 0`, and then `F = 0` by property 1.

**What each definition will need when its parts are replaced.**

- **`TStructuredDGCat` and `IsEventuallyCoconnective`.** A t-structure in which every object is bounded below makes property 1 empty. Then uniqueness fails and the "for all" half becomes false. Needed: an example of a DG category with an object that is not bounded below (in `Vect`, the direct sum of `k[n]` over all `n ≥ 0`), and the fact that `IndCohNilp S` carries the t-structure for which `Ψ` is t-exact.
- **`DGFun`.** It must be exactly the continuous `k`-linear functors. If non-continuous functors are allowed, the uniqueness of Step C fails. Needed: the laws that link `obj`, `comp` and `id`, and an example of a functor that is not in the class.
- **`DGFun.IsEquivalence`.** If `DGNatIso` is always inhabited, every functor is an equivalence and the theorem reduces to its existence half. Needed: `DGNatIso` is reflexive, symmetric and transitive; the identity is an equivalence; the zero functor of `Vect` is not; `IsEquivalence` is invariant under `DGNatIso`.
- **`IsLanglandsFunctor`.** If `MapsEquiv` is always inhabited, property 2 is empty. Needed: the example of the plan's replacement contract (for `J` the identity, `MapsEquiv` is the adjunction equivalence); an example where `MapsEquiv` is empty (in `Vect`: `H` and `J` the identity, `T` zero); invariance of `IsLanglandsFunctor` under `DGNatIso`; and one explicit `Setting`, so that the theorem is not empty.
