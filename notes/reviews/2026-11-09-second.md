- A subagent received only this text and the Tau Ceti definitions behind the `Setting` fields
  (`ReductiveCommHopfAlgCat`, `reductiveCommHopfAlgProperty_iff`, `SplitMaximalTorus`,
  `IsPositiveRoot`, `IsSimpleRoot`, `Pinning`, `InvertibleSheaf`,
  `InvertibleSheaf.tensorProduct`, `Scheme.relativeDifferentials`), without docstrings.
- It was told to open no file and to use no tool. Its only tool call was the hand-back of its
  report.

Mapping back to the real names, its reading was this:

- **The final theorem.** For every field `k` of characteristic zero that is algebraically
  closed, and every `S : Setting k`, two things hold:
  - some `F : LinFun (DmodHalf S) (IndCohNilp S).toModule` satisfies both properties;
  - every `F` that satisfies both properties is an equivalence.

  The two properties are:
  - for every compact `c`, the object `F.obj c` is eventually coconnective;
  - `LinIso (F.comp (Psi S)) (LCoarse S)` is inhabited.

  `comp` is in diagrammatic order, so this is `Ψ ∘ F ≅ L_coarse`. `IsEquivalence` asks for
  a `G` with `LinIso (F.comp G) (id)` and `LinIso (G.comp F) (id)`, which pairs correctly in
  diagrammatic order. No conclusion is stored as a hypothesis or as a field.
- **The genuine data.** The curve fields mean a nonempty, connected, smooth, proper curve over
  `k`. `G` is a connected reductive group. `Pinning` is a real pinning: a split maximal torus,
  a Borel subgroup containing it (the ideal inclusion goes the right way), and a basis vector
  of each simple root space. The theta characteristic is a line bundle `L` with an
  isomorphism `L ⊗ L ≅ Ω_{X/k}` in `X.Modules`, given as data. Only `dualIdentification` rests
  on an opaque.
- **Its hack tests.**
  - If `RootDatumDuality` is empty, `Setting k` is empty and the theorem is vacuous.
  - If `LinIso` is always inhabited and one of the predicates is trivial, the theorem is easy.
  - If `LinIso` is empty, the theorem is false for every inhabited `Setting`.
  - The `NonemptyType` carriers forbid only empty categories and empty functor types.
- **Oddities.** `MonoidalDGCat.toDGCat` is unused (finding L1). No law relates `obj`, `comp`,
  `id` and `LinIso`. The other `Setting` data reach the theorem only through opaque maps.
- **Provability.** Neither the theorem nor its negation can be proved from the text.

I compared this reading with the TeX, not with the names (next section). It agrees with the
first report's blind reading on every point, and it adds L1.

## The statement against the papers

**Standing hypotheses.**

- [GLC1] §0.1.1 (`2405.03599v3/src/functor.tex`:715–719) takes `k` of characteristic 0, `X`
  smooth and complete, `G` connected reductive, and `Ǧ` the Langlands dual.
- Algebraic closure and connectedness of `X` are the conventions [GLC1] relies on: [AGKRRV]
  §14.1.1 (`text.tex`:15121–15124) and [GLC2] §0.4.1 (`2405.03648v3/src/Loc.tex`:1495–1502).
  `AGENTS.md` fixes the same scope.
- `Setting` (`Conjecture.lean`:44–64) matches these:
  - smooth of relative dimension 1, proper and connected;
  - Tau Ceti reductivity includes smoothness, geometric connectedness and a trivial unipotent
    radical (`TauCeti/Algebra/AlgebraicGroup/Reductive/Basic.lean`:63–97 and 169–170 at the
    pin).
- The pinning and the theta characteristic are data that [GLC1] fixes in §1.3
  (`functor.tex`:1509–1512 for the square root; §1.3.6, `functor.tex`:1616–1636, uses the
  simple-root data). The statement holds for every choice of them, which is the right reading
  of "fix a choice".
- Verdict for the geometric fields: *same object, different presentation*. Tau Ceti also has
  `InvertibleSheaf.relativeDifferentials`
  (`TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Modules/Differentials/Rank.lean`:112), which would put the
  isomorphism inside `InvertibleSheaf X`. That is the same full subcategory, so the meaning is
  the same.

**The logical form.** The statement is equivalent to [GLC1] Conj. 1.6.7, given what [GLC1]
proves. The argument:

- [GLC1] Cor. 1.6.5 (`functor.tex`:1833–1850) gives a unique colimit-preserving `𝕃_G`
  with the two properties, stated for plain functors and a plain natural isomorphism.
  Prop. 1.7.2 (`functor.tex`:1939–1944) gives `𝕃_G` a unique `QCoh(LS_Ǧ)`-linear structure
  for which `Ψ ∘ 𝕃_G ≅ 𝕃_coarse` is linear. So the existence conjunct (`Conjecture.lean`:116)
  is a theorem of [GLC1], resting on Thm. 1.6.2 (`functor.tex`:1808–1812).
- Take any linear `F` with the two properties. Forgetting linearity, its underlying functor has
  the properties of Cor. 1.6.5, so it is isomorphic to `𝕃_G` by uniqueness.
- The forgetful functor from module categories to DG categories reflects equivalences, so `F`
  is a linear equivalence exactly when `𝕃_G` is an equivalence.
- Therefore the universal conjunct (`Conjecture.lean`:117–118) is equivalent to Conj. 1.6.7
  (`functor.tex`:1861–1863).
- Directions agree with the paper:
  - `Psi` goes from `IndCoh_Nilp` to `QCoh` ([GLC1] §1.6.3, `functor.tex`:1818–1826).
  - `LCoarse` goes from `Dmod_{1/2}` to `QCoh` ([GLC1] §1.4.5, `functor.tex`:1719–1729).
  - Compactness is tested in the source; eventual coconnectivity is tested in the target, for
    the t-structure of `IndCoh_Nilp`.
- Morphisms of DG categories are colimit-preserving ([AGKRRV] §0.9.4, `text.tex`:2143–2146).
- Eventual coconnectivity is membership in some `C^{≥ -n}` ([AGKRRV] §0.9.5,
  `text.tex`:2178–2181), which the docstring at `Enhanced.lean`:92–93 states.
