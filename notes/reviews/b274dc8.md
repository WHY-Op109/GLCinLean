## Answers to the four questions on `b274dc8`

**1. Are the descriptions right, and do they match the sources?** Yes, with the corrections above. The orientation of the kernel twist is consistent: `det_{Bun_G}` is normalized against the trivial bundle ([GLC1] §1.1.1, `functor.tex` 1320–1325), `det_{Gr_G}` is the relative determinant of `𝔤_P` against `𝔤_{P^0}` ([GLys] §5.2.1, `text.tex` 4856–4883), so on the Hecke stack `h_out^*det ⊗ h_in^*det^{-1}` is the pullback of `det_{Gr_G}`. This agrees with `Satake.criticalDeterminantGerbe` and `Satake.factorizationSphericalCategory`. For μ_2 the inverse of a gerbe is canonically the gerbe itself, so the opposite orientation would give an equivalent category; one orientation must still be fixed for the composition constraints.

**2. Is the construction of the twisted pushforward sound?** Yes. The twisted `f^!` is a limit of functors over test schemes with a trivialization. On each term it is the untwisted `f^!` through the untwisting equivalences, and this has the left adjoint `f_!`. The transition functors are `!`-pullbacks followed by tensoring with a rank-one local system. The base-change condition holds for the pullbacks by [GaitsgoryAtiyahBott] Cor. 1.5.4 (`Tamagawa.tex` 1908–1930). For the local systems it is formal: tensoring with an invertible object is an equivalence that commutes with `f^!`, so its inverse commutes with the left adjoint. So the general projection formula is not needed for the construction. `Infinity.adjointableDiagram_limitAdjunction` then gives the adjoint on the limit, and independence of the cover follows from uniqueness of adjoints. The `deps` reach étale descent (`twistedCrystals`, with `rightCrystals_hDescent`; [Crystals] Prop. 3.2.2, `Crystalstext.tex` 1902–1911), the untwisted pushforward with base change, and the gluing of adjunctions. There is no circular route. The gaps are one level below, in `twistedCrystals` (points 1–3). The generality is the natural one: étale μ_n-gerbes are exactly [GLC1] §1.1.3, and pseudo-proper maps are exactly where the untwisted `f_!` exists on all objects. A shorter construction exists and may be worth a note in the node: over the trivialized test schemes of [GLys] §1.7.2 no cover has to be chosen.

**3. Is it still the paper's action, and does pinning independence still hold?** Yes. The paper's integrated action is `M ↦ C_c(Ran, a · (M ⊗ ω_Ran))` ([GLC2] Remark H.6.9, `Loc.tex` 38065–38079). The local action is the ind-proper pushforward along the Hecke stack over `Bun_G × Ran`, and `C_c(Ran, −)` is the pseudo-proper pushforward along `Ran`. Their composite is the twisted pushforward along `h_out`. Composition, diagonals and factorization need composition, base change and the projection formula of the twisted pushforward, which the node lists, and the compatibility of the gerbe comparison with composition of modifications and factorization, which `Satake.criticalTwist_matchesBunDeterminant` states. The lax-unital structure that Remark H.6.9 requires for the associativity constraint is given here by the composition maps of Hecke stacks over `X^{I₁ ⊔ I₂}`, including colliding points; the `desc` does not name it, and this was so before the commit. One compatibility is not listed: on `Bun_G × Ran → Bun_G` the twisted pushforward is the identity tensored with `C_c(Ran, −)`. `Satake.dualIdentificationChangePinningIso` is not affected: a change of pinning changes only the naive Satake functor, and the isomorphism of Hecke actions is obtained by composing with the fixed spherical action.

**4. Is the changed dependency enough for the example?** Yes for the pushforward. For the trivial group `det_{Bun_1}` is the trivial line, so the gerbe has a trivialization `t`, and the theorem gives `(h_out)_{!,𝒢} ≃ U_t^{-1} ∘ p_! ∘ U_{p^*t}`. The general form of the theorem is the one needed, because in Lean the gerbe is the root gerbe of the determinant bundle and not literally the trivial gerbe. The full claim `V ⋆ M ≃ p_!V ⊗ M` also uses the compatibility of the twisted `!`-pullback and of the twisted tensor product with untwisting. The first is in `DModules.extraordinaryPullback`; the second is not yet in `DModules.twistedCrystals` (point 4).

## Review of `92dc3d7`

**Does the route cover every prestack locally almost of finite type?** Yes, apart from points 5–7. The route is the paper's proof of [Crystals] Prop. 2.4.4 (`Crystalstext.tex` 1561–1628), step by step: reduction to affine schemes (Cor. 2.2.4 and 2.3.9), the completion `Y` of a smooth `Z` along `X`, descent along `Y → Y_dR` (Lemmas 2.1.7 and 2.3.11), the Čech nerve, and the two localization sequences. The paper's proofs of these inputs do not use Prop. 2.4.4, so there is no circle.

**Is `X_dR → Y_dR` an isomorphism?** Yes, and `Derived.formalCompletion` is enough for it. With `Y = Z ×_{Z_dR} X_dR`, a point of `Y_dR` over `S` is a point of `Y` over the reduced classical `S`, which is a point of `X` over it, because there the map `Z → Z_dR` is the identity. The same computation gives the Čech nerve: the (n+1)-fold product of `Y` over `Y_dR = X_dR` is `Z^{n+1} ×_{(Z^{n+1})_dR} X_dR`.

**Are the other inputs general enough?** Yes. `DModules.leftToRightCrystals` is natural on all prestacks locally almost of finite type. `DModules.crystals_descent_of_classicallyFormallySmooth` holds for every classically formally smooth prestack locally almost of finite type (point 5 is the missing check that the completion is one). `Sheaves.qcoh_formalCompletion_localization` and `Sheaves.indcoh_formalCompletion_localization` hold for every closed subscheme of a scheme of finite type, with the compatibility under Υ; this covers `X` in `Z^i` ([DGIndschemes] Prop. 7.1.3 and 7.4.5). `Sheaves.upsilon_isEquivalence_of_smooth` covers `Z^i` and the open complement. `Derived.formalCompletion` covers closed subschemes.
