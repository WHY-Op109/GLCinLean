## Findings (none blocks)

- **N1 (should fix). The composite to Gr_G is birational only for minimal coset
  representatives.**
  - `AffineGrassmannian/BottSamelson.toml:4` (`Hecke.bottSamelsonResolution`) builds, for each w
    in the affine Weyl group, a Bott–Samelson map onto the I-Schubert variety of w in LG/I. It
    then says to "compose with the projection to Gr_G for I-Schubert varieties there".
  - `Satake/Spherical.toml:18` (`Satake.icStalkParity_complex`), step (3), says "the map is
    birational, so the IC sheaf ... occurs exactly once and unshifted".
  - The composite LG/I → Gr_G is an isomorphism over the open I-orbit only when w has minimal
    length in its coset w·W_f. For other w, the generic fibre is a positive-dimensional finite
    Schubert variety, and the IC sheaf occurs several times, with shifts.
  - The parity conclusion is not affected, because a direct summand of an even complex is even.
    Only the sentence in step (3) is false as written. Say "w minimal in w·W_f" in one of the two
    nodes. (Alternatively use JMW Prop. 4.11 with I_n equal to the finite-type subset, which maps
    directly to Gr_G.)
- **N2 (nit). "Parity" versus "even".** `BottSamelson.toml:11` defines a parity complex as one
  whose restrictions have "degrees of one parity". In JMW a parity complex is a sum of an even and
  an odd complex (`paritysheaves_a.tex`, §2). The Bott–Samelson pushforward of the unshifted
  constant sheaf is in fact *even*: the proof of Prop. 4.11 (`:3089–3100`) starts from a
  skyscraper and applies functors that preserve evenness. Step (3) of `Spherical.toml:18` needs
  "even" to get degrees ≡ dim Gr^λ. Say "even" in `BottSamelson.toml:11`.
- **N3 (nit). Prop. 4.10 uses more than Prop. 2.36.** `BottSamelson.toml:11` says "proper even
  maps preserve parity (Proposition 2.36), hence pushforward and pullback along the projections
  ... preserve parity (Proposition 4.10)". In JMW only the pushforward half uses Prop. 2.36
  (`:3000–3002`). The pullback half uses that the projection is smooth, so π^! ≅ π^*[2d]
  (`:3003–3005`). Prop. 4.10 also needs K of finite type (`:2981–2984`). Neither point changes the
  route.
- **N4 (nit). Wording of `Hecke.action_localUnital`** (`Hecke/Action.toml:39`).
  - "no source writes the proof" is a claim about all sources. Say "no source found".
  - [GLC2] defines a local Ran-unital action as an action of a sheaf of monoidal categories on
    the unital Ran space (§H.6.1, `Loc.tex:37956–37981`; §H.4, `:37550`). The node calls the
    first input "Rep(H) as a unital monoidal factorization category". Factorization is more than
    §H.6.1 asks. This is harmless, but naming §H.4 would be more precise.
- **N5 (unsure; outside the three items). The new rigidity route.**
  `Hecke/Representations.toml:25` (`Hecke.unitalIndependentRepresentations_rigid`) says "No source states this for the
  independent category: [GLC1] §1.2.2 asserts rigidity of Rep(H)_Ran without proof", and proposes
  a route in which the compact generators are dualizable "because Rep(H) is rigid and Dmod(X^I) is
  compactly generated by dualizable objects".
  - [AGKRRV] §11.3.3 (`2010.01906v2/src/text.tex:12095–12150`) proves that C_Ran is compactly
    generated and rigid for X proper and C compactly generated and rigid. The dual of a generator
    ins_{I→J}(V ⊗ F) is ins_{I→J}(V^∨ ⊗ D(F)), with D the Verdier duality. So for Rep(H)_Ran
    there is a source, and the GLC1 sentence is not the only one.
  - The duality there is given by Verdier duality. It is not dualizability of compact objects of
    Dmod(X^J) for its pointwise !-tensor product. (For that product, δ_x is compact, but I believe
    it is not dualizable.) The phrase "Dmod(X^I) is compactly generated by dualizable objects" is
    therefore at best ambiguous.
  - I did not check whether the passage to the independent category keeps rigidity. Cite
    [AGKRRV] §11.3.3 for Rep(H)_Ran, and state the monoidal structure in which the duals exist.
