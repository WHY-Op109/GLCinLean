### Notes on the verdicts

- **`Constructible.DerivedCategory`.** The new condition on the maps is sufficient, on standard mathematics computed here. Let f be proper, with the preimage of each stratum T′ a union of strata, and f^{-1}(T′) → T′ locally the projection F × U → U, with the strata of f^{-1}(U) the products F_i × U. Proper base change reduces f_*K, for K in D^b_S, to the restriction of K to f^{-1}(T′). Over a contractible U this restriction is built from the extensions by zero of local systems on the F_i × U, and each of these is pulled back from F_i, so f_*K is constant on U with fibre the compactly supported cohomology of F, of finite rank. The *-pullback needs only the first condition, and the !-pullback is D f^* D, which needs both stratifications good. The new counterexample is right: {x² = y} is closed in P¹ × A¹ (in the chart u = 1/x its equation is 1 = yu²), smooth and connected, its fibre over y has two points for y ≠ 0 and one point for y = 0, and the projection is a trivial fibration whose preimage of the one stratum is the whole space. An equivariant map f : X → Y between orbit stratifications of one group H is stratified in the node's sense: over an orbit H/K with a local section s of H → H/K, the map (u, x) ↦ s(u)x identifies U × f^{-1}(y) with f^{-1}(U) and carries U × (O ∩ f^{-1}(y)) to O ∩ f^{-1}(U) for every orbit O. So the users in `plans/` (the Bott–Samelson pushforward) are covered. The clauses "since L+G and I act on a finite Schubert union through quotients of finite type", "the !-restriction of the constant sheaf" and "The !-half of the condition always holds" are right. "Hack answer" test for "good": the node gives examples (Whitney and orbit stratifications) and a stratification that is not good (the umbrella), so the predicate is neither empty nor always true.
- **`DModules.deRhamPushforward`, `DModules.starPullback`, `DModules.verdierDuality`.** [DG2] §5.3.5 says: for smooth f the functor f_{dR,*} has a left adjoint f^*_dR, and f^*_dR ≃ f^![−2n] for constant relative dimension n (`Finiteness.tex` 5075–5081); it also gives D(f^*_dR M) ≃ f^!(DM) on coherent M (5088–5091). So the three nodes now agree with each other and with the source: the smooth adjunction is stated in `deRhamPushforward`, `starPullback` cites it, and `verdierDuality` uses it and no longer needs `starPullback`. The proof route named in `verdierDuality` works: for M coherent, Hom(D(f^![−2d]M), N) = Γ_dR(f^!M[−2d] ⊗^! N) = Γ_dR(M ⊗^! f_*N)[−2d] = Hom(DM, f_*N[−2d]) = Hom(f^!DM, N), by the duality formula ([DG2] §5.3.1, 4998–5007), the projection formula (§5.2.6, 4943–4952) and the smooth adjunction. The new `deps` of `verdierDuality` (`DModules.deRhamPushforward`, `DModules.shriekTensor`) create no cycle. The definition of D on a stack ([DG2] §7.3.4, 6244–6253) uses g^*_dR for smooth charts g, which is now available without `starPullback`.

### What the Tau Ceti declarations say (reading of the Lean text without docstrings)

Notation of the Lean text: `k` is a field in `Type u`, `X` is a scheme in the same universe with a structure map `X ↘ Spec k` (`[X.Over (Spec (.of k))]`). `LineBundleClass X` is the type of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves of `𝒪_X`-modules (`LineBundle/Class.lean` 50–51), a commutative group under tensor product (line 142), with inverse the dual (95–104). `Additive (LineBundleClass X)` is the same group written additively. This is the Picard group of the node.

1. **`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.InvertibleSheaf.eulerDegree`** and **`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.eulerDegree`** (`LineBundle/Degree.lean` 74–76 and 130–132). For an invertible sheaf `L`, the integer `(dim_k H⁰(X, L) − dim_k H¹(X, L)) − (dim_k H⁰(X, 𝒪_X) − dim_k H¹(X, 𝒪_X))`. The dimensions are `finrank`, so an infinite-dimensional group counts as 0. The value depends only on the isomorphism class. No hypothesis on `X`.
2. **`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.eulerDegreeHom`** (`WeilDivisor/Scheme/PicZero.lean` 79–81; hypotheses on lines 68–71). Hypotheses: `X` is integral and Noetherian; the local ring at every point of codimension one is a discrete valuation ring; every point has codimension at most one (`Fact (∀ y, coheight y ≤ 1)`); `X` is proper over `k`; `H¹(X, 𝒪_X)` is finite-dimensional over `k`. Statement: the map `a ↦ eulerDegree k a` is a homomorphism of additive groups `Additive (LineBundleClass X) →+ ℤ`. Additivity is `LineBundleClass.eulerDegree_mul` (`WeilDivisor/Scheme/EulerCharacteristic.lean` 343).
3. **`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.picZero`** (`WeilDivisor/Scheme/PicZero.lean` 92–93). Under the same hypotheses: the kernel of `eulerDegreeHom`, an additive subgroup of `Additive (LineBundleClass X)`. `picZero_eq_ker` (line 97) holds by definition, and `mem_picZero_iff` (102) says a class is in it exactly when its degree is 0.
4. **`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.eulerDegree_toLineBundleClass_ofPoint_of_section`** (`RationalPoint/Degree.lean` 91–98; hypotheses on lines 81–84). Hypotheses: as in 2, with "every point has codimension at most one" as the explicit argument `hX`. Given a morphism `s : Spec k ⟶ X` with `s ≫ (X ↘ Spec k) = 𝟙`, a point `y` of `Spec k`, and a point `x₀` of codimension one of `X` with `x₀ = s y`. Statement: the class of the invertible sheaf `𝒪_X(x₀)` of the Weil divisor `1·x₀` (`SchemeWeilDivisor.toLineBundleClass hX (WeilDivisor.ofPoint x₀)`) has Euler degree 1.
5. **`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.eulerDegree_relativeDifferentials`** (`Modules/Differentials/Canonical.lean` 309–317; hypotheses on lines 58–59 and 212–217). Hypotheses: `X` is integral, Noetherian and separated; discrete valuation rings in codimension one; the sheaf `X.relativeDifferentials k` is invertible; `hF`: the function field of `X` is a function field of one variable over `k` (finite over `k(x)` for some transcendental `x`, `FieldTheory/FunctionField/Basic.lean` 51–52); `hk`: `k` is integrally closed in the function field; `hdim`: every point has codimension at most one; `hex`: the valuative criterion of existence for `X ↘ Spec k`; `X` is proper over `k`. Statement: the Euler degree of `Ω_{X/k}` equals `2 * X.genus k − 2` in ℤ.
6. **`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.genus`** (`Cohomology/Genus.lean` 64–67): `X.genus k = dim_k H¹(X, 𝒪_X)`, a natural number, defined only when this space is finite-dimensional. **`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.relativeDifferentials`** (`Modules/Differentials/Basic.lean` 85–87): for a scheme `X` over `Spec R`, `R` a commutative ring, the sheafification of `U ↦ Ω[Γ(X, U)⁄R]`, with the universal property `(Ω_{X/R} ⟶ M) ≃ Der_R(𝒪_X, M)` (`relativeDifferentialsHomEquiv`, line 101).
7. **`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.InvertibleSheaf.high_degree_hypotheses_of_smooth`** (`Curves/HighDegree.lean` 50–66; hypotheses on lines 43–45). Hypotheses: `X` is proper over `k`, smooth of relative dimension 1 over `k`, and geometrically connected over `k`. Statement: `X` is integral, and then: discrete valuation rings in codimension one, every point has codimension at most one, the function field of `X` is a function field of one variable over `k`, and `k` is integrally closed in it.

No conclusion is moved into a hypothesis. The hypothesis "`H¹(X, 𝒪_X)` is finite-dimensional" is true for every proper curve and is proved upstream (`finiteDimensional_cohomology_one_trivial_of_isProper`, `WeilDivisor/Scheme/RiemannRoch/Finiteness.lean` 60). The hypothesis `hk` is needed: when the field of constants is larger than `k`, the formula `2g − 2` over `k` is false. The hypothesis `hex` follows from properness.

### `GLCinLean.Curves.picard_degree`: *same object, different presentation*

TOML: `plans/Roadmap/JacobianChallenge/Jacobian.toml` line 6: "The degree deg : Pic X → ℤ of a smooth proper geometrically connected curve over a field, a group homomorphism with deg 𝒪(x) = 1 for a rational point x, and Pic⁰ X = ker deg as an abstract group."

- **Group homomorphism on the Picard group with values in ℤ.** Yes: item 2 above. The group is the group of isomorphism classes of line bundles.
- **It is the usual degree.** The definition is `χ(L) − χ(𝒪_X)` with the Euler characteristic cut off above degree 1. `LineBundleClass.eulerDegree_toLineBundleClass` (`WeilDivisor/Scheme/EulerCharacteristic.lean` 325–336) proves `deg 𝒪_X(D) = Σ_y D(y)·[κ(y) : k]` for every Weil divisor `D`, and `SchemeWeilDivisor.toLineBundleClass_surjective` (`WeilDivisor/Scheme/Picard.lean` 230) says every class is some `𝒪_X(D)`. So on the curves of the theorem the Euler degree is the degree of a divisor. The junk value of `finrank` plays no role there.
- **deg 𝒪(x) = 1 for a rational point.** Yes: item 4. The sign is right: `RationalPoint/PointSeparation.lean` 86–92 shows that the class of the divisor `−x` is the class of the ideal sheaf of `x` (`kerSheafIsoNegPoint`, `RationalPoint/IdealSheaf.lean` 132), so the divisor `+x` gives `𝒪(x)`. The rational point is a section `Spec k ⟶ X`, and its image must be given as a point of codimension one.
- **Pic⁰ is the kernel.** Yes, by definition: item 3.
- **Hypotheses for a smooth proper geometrically connected curve over a field.** Each one is discharged by a declaration at the pin: integrality, the discrete valuation rings and codimension at most one by `InvertibleSheaf.high_degree_hypotheses_of_smooth` (item 7); `H¹(X, 𝒪_X)` finite-dimensional by `finiteDimensional_cohomology_one_trivial_of_smooth` (`WeilDivisor/Scheme/RiemannRoch/Finiteness.lean` 79–91, which needs only "connected"); Noetherian from properness (`LocallyOfFiniteType.isLocallyNoetherian` and `quasiCompact_iff_compactSpace`, as `RationalPoint/PointSeparation.lean` 67–69 does); the image of a section is closed by `isClosed_singleton_of_section` (`RationalPoint/Basic.lean` 274) and so of codimension one by `coheight_eq_of_isClosed_of_smoothOfRelativeDimension` (`Morphisms/Smooth/RelativeDimension.lean` 120–123). `LineBundleClass.eulerDegree_mul_of_smooth` (`Curves/Degree.lean` 55–71) does this derivation for additivity, and `RationalPoint/PointSeparation.lean` 64–84 does it for a rational point.
- **Degenerate cases.** A curve without a rational point: the homomorphism and Pic⁰ need no point, and the statement on `𝒪(x)` is empty, as in the node. Genus 0: nothing special; on ℙ¹ the degree is the usual one (Tau Ceti `4d91513e`, `LineBundle/ProjectiveLine.lean` 78: `eulerDegree (𝒪(n)) = n`). `X = Spec k` satisfies the hypotheses of items 2 and 3 with the zero homomorphism; it is not a curve of the plan and does no harm.
- **"Hack answer" test.** The homomorphism is not trivial on a curve with a rational point: `SchemeWeilDivisor.eulerDegreeHom_surjective` (`WeilDivisor/Scheme/PicZero.lean` 214) shows it is surjective, and `picQuotientPicZeroAddEquivInt` (225) gives `Pic X / Pic⁰ X ≃ ℤ`. The hypotheses are not contradictory, since smooth proper curves satisfy them.

What differs from the node is only the presentation: the Picard group is written additively; the degree is defined by Euler characteristics and then identified with the degree of a divisor; the hypotheses are those of a regular proper curve, which is more general than smooth; the smooth case is not one declaration. Nothing that the node states is missing, so no narrower roadmap node remains.

### `GLCinLean.Curves.degree_canonical`: *same object, different presentation*

TOML: `plans/Roadmap/JacobianChallenge/SerreDuality.toml` line 12: "For a smooth proper geometrically connected curve X of genus g over a field, deg ω_X = 2g − 2."

- **The statement.** Item 5 above: `deg Ω_{X/k} = 2g − 2`, with the degree of item 1.
- **ω_X.** It is `X.relativeDifferentials k`, the sheaf of Kähler differentials with its universal property (item 6). This is the object that `Curves.ThetaCharacteristic` names for the canonical bundle (`plans/GLCinLean/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/Theta.toml` line 6), so the theorem is about the right sheaf.
- **The genus.** `g = dim_k H¹(X, 𝒪_X)` (item 6). The same file proves `dim_k H⁰(X, Ω_{X/k}) = g` (`finrank_cohomology_zero_relativeDifferentials`, `Modules/Differentials/Canonical.lean` 266–273), so the two usual definitions of the genus agree here. The node does not say which one it means; both give the same number.
- **The value is an integer.** The left side is in ℤ, so `2 * X.genus k − 2` is read in ℤ with the genus cast from ℕ. For genus 0 it is −2, not 0. A subtraction in ℕ would make the theorem false for ℙ¹, whose genus is 0 at the pin (`Cohomology/ProjectiveLine/Basic.lean` 755).
- **Hypotheses for a smooth proper geometrically connected curve over a field.** Integrality, the discrete valuation rings, `hdim`, `hF` and `hk` by `InvertibleSheaf.high_degree_hypotheses_of_smooth` (item 7); invertibility of `Ω_{X/k}` by `isInvertible_relativeDifferentials` (`Modules/Differentials/Rank.lean` 101–105); Noetherian and separated from properness; `hex` from `UniversallyClosed.eq_valuativeCriterion` (as `WeilDivisor/Scheme/PicZero.lean` 255–258 does). All are at the pin.
- **Degenerate cases.** Genus 0 gives −2 (above). A curve without a rational point is allowed: the theorem asks for no point. A curve that is connected but not geometrically connected is excluded by `hk`, which is right.
- **"Hack answer" test.** The theorem is not empty: ℙ¹ and every smooth proper geometrically connected curve satisfy its hypotheses.

What differs from the node is again the presentation: the hypotheses are in function-field form, and no declaration states the smooth case. Nothing that the node states is missing.
