# Which groups belong in the Ran comparison?

**I recommend option 2.** State both equivalences for every finite-type affine group scheme H over the project's characteristic-zero field. Give compact generation and rigidity of `Rep(H) = QCoh(BH)` their own theorem, proved using [DG2]. Keep the definition of `Rep(H)` independent of that theorem. The current representation-category plan promises this property only for reductive H. The argument below supplies it in the full generality of Raskin’s two group results. The current Satake construction uses Ǧ, and the point example uses the trivial group. ## What the two proofs use

- **`Ran.Representations.equivCollisionLimit`.** [RaskinCPSII] §9.1 fixes an affine algebraic group. Lemma 9.8.1 compares the two categories. In Proposition A.4.1 the rigid category used for an affine target is the category of modules over its coordinate algebra. In Appendix A.5, Step 3, the Čech terms **and their limit** `Rep(H)` must be dualizable as DG categories. Compact generation of `Rep(H)` suffices for that last step. There is also a direct source for its dualizability, since BH is a classical QCA stack locally of finite type. Requiring the full hypothesis of §6.15 on H here would overstate what this step uses. [Raskin II, Lemma 9.8.1 and Appendix A.2–A.5](https://www.samraskin.net/cpsii.pdf#page=94) · [DG2, Theorem 4.3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5351v3)
- **`Ran.Representations.linearFunctorEquivComodule`.** [RaskinCPSI] §6.15 assumes a compactly generated symmetric monoidal DG category with compact unit and tensor duals for all compact objects. Corollary 6.18.2 gives self-duality over $D(X^I)$. Section 6.19 specializes to representations of an affine algebraic group. Proposition 6.22.1 identifies the regular object via the right adjoint of the forgetful functor, and Lemma 6.23.2 proves comonadicity after tensoring with the target module category. Lemma 6.32.1 then gives the equivalence by evaluating a functor on the regular object. Thus §6.15 belongs on the categorical lemmas. The group specialization also uses its forgetful functor and the geometry of $B(H^n)$. [Raskin I, §§6.15–6.23 and Lemma 6.32.1](https://www.samraskin.net/cpsi.pdf#page=43)

## Why every finite-type affine H qualifies

1. The point atlas makes BH quasi-compact. Its diagonal is affine of finite type, so BH is QCA. By [DG2] Theorem 1.4.2, derived global sections preserve colimits. This gives a compact unit. For a finite-dimensional rational representation V, mapping out of V is computed by applying derived global sections to $V^\vee \otimes -$. Thus every such V is compact and has a tensor dual. [DG2, Theorem 1.4.2 and Corollary 1.4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5351v3#page=22)
2. Generation needs a separate argument. Apply [DG2] Lemma 2.6.3 to the action of H on a point. The heart generates `QCoh(BH)` under stable colimits. Every rational comodule in the heart is the directed union of its finite-dimensional subcomodules. Tau Ceti proves that each vector lies in one, and sums give directedness. Equivalently, [DG2] Corollary 1.4.11 gives generation by the coherent heart, which on BH consists of the finite-dimensional representations. The compact objects are their thick closure and hence have tensor duals. This supplies both Raskin's §6.15 hypothesis and the sufficient criterion in the planned `Infinity.rigidMonoidalCategory`. The remark after [DG2] Theorem 4.3.1 leaves compact generation of general QCA `QCoh` open there. The identification of the generators on BH is essential. [DG2, Corollary 1.4.11 and Lemma 2.6.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5351v3#page=23) · [Tau Ceti, finite subcomodules at the pin](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/086028a6d8d186715b52fac7f3e7b23175ed2dc9/TauCeti/Algebra/Coalgebra/Subcomodule/Finite.lean#L106) · [Rigidity criterion](../../plans/Roadmap/EnhancedDerivedSheaves/Rigid.toml)

## What option 4 gains and costs

Claude's proposal is logically sound if the property is proved independently for each use. Assuming compact generation and rigidity in a helper that *concludes a Ran equivalence* does not assume its conclusion. The breach of `AGENTS.md` would be to put the property into the data of a Langlands setting, or to count a conditional comparison as proving Raskin's assertion for every H without discharging its premise. The project's two nodes supply equivalences as data. At that interface every allowed H qualifies, so asking the caller for this proof adds a redundant hypothesis. Mathlib illustrates the distinction at the pin. Its `preservesLimitIso` takes preservation of the relevant limit as a hypothesis, and a separate instance supplies preservation for every right adjoint. [Repository rule and owner decisions of Nov. 11 and 13](../../STATUS.md) · [Mathlib comparison](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/e8ba86fa9744dd8d90f48c1752bc735f61a4ca81/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Limits/Preserves/Limits.lean#L53) · [Mathlib instance](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/e8ba86fa9744dd8d90f48c1752bc735f61a4ca81/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Adjunction/Limits.lean#L346)

Option 4 could deliver the reductive comparisons earlier. At the Tau Ceti pin, `TauCeti.reductiveCommHopfAlgProperty_iff_linearlyReductive` takes a finite-type commutative Hopf algebra over a characteristic-zero field and a geometric connectedness hypothesis. The forward implication is already available as `TauCeti.reductiveCommHopfAlgProperty.linearlyReductive`, and `TauCeti.Comodule.isCompletelyReducible_of_reductive` covers arbitrary rational comodules. These are theorems about the heart. To use them here one still has to identify the unbounded derived category of that heart with `QCoh(BH)`, then show that finite-dimensional simples compactly generate it. Semisimplicity supplies the splitting of complexes, and finite dimensionality makes mapping from a simple commute with direct sums. [Tau Ceti, complete reducibility at the pin](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/086028a6d8d186715b52fac7f3e7b23175ed2dc9/TauCeti/Algebra/AlgebraicGroup/Reductive/CompleteReducibility.lean#L90) · [DG2, Lemma 6.4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.5351v3#page=74)

Option 4 would let work on the reductive comparisons proceed before the QCA theorem. At project commit `c02ca99`, neither comparison has a declared dependency path to `Sheaves.qcaGlobalSections_continuous`. But `Satake.naiveFactorizationFunctor` already reaches it through the weight functors and D-module compact generation, and `Spectral.localization` reaches it through the same compact-generation theorem. Option 4 would add a second proof of the representation-category property to advance these two comparisons. I would keep the categorical proof lemmas reusable and spend that effort on the all-group theorem already served by the QCA plan. [QCA plan](../../plans/GLCinLean/AlgebraicGeometry/Sheaves/QCAFiniteness.toml) · [D-module compact generation](../../plans/GLCinLean/AlgebraicGeometry/DModules/CompactGeneration.toml) · [Spectral localization](../../plans/GLCinLean/GeometricLanglands/Spectral/Localization.toml)

**Other options.** Options 1 and 5 exclude even the additive group in characteristic zero, for which the proof above applies despite the failure of complete reducibility. Option 3 has the right scope, but passing to a Levi factor also requires control of derived invariants for the unipotent radical. Option 6 leaves the nonreductive case without a planned proof. A missing option is to split the hypotheses by result, using DG-category dualizability for the geometric comparison and §6.15 for the comodule proof, then deriving both all-group statements. This is compatible with option 2 and explains where the conditional lemmas would be useful. A further independent check is Hall–Rydh's direct compact-generation theorem for classifying stacks, which in characteristic zero even gives irreducible representations as compact generators. [Hall–Rydh, Theorem A(c)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.1890#page=1)
