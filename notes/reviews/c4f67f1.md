## Item 7: the new planned theorem as evidence (`Coarse.toml` lines 31–36, node `Langlands.isEquivalence_iff_of_isLanglandsFunctor`)

**Agree.** The claim: if two continuous functors `F` and `F′` both satisfy `IsLanglandsFunctor`, then they are naturally equivalent as functors of ∞-categories, and `F` is an equivalence if and only if `F′` is. This is true, and each step of the sketch works. Write `D = Dmod_½(Bun_G)`, `I = IndCoh_Nilp(LS_Ǧ)`, `Q = QCoh(LS_Ǧ)`, `R = Rep(Ǧ)_Ran`.

1. "the two mapping-space equivalences give `Maps(Loc V, Ψ (F M)) ≃ Maps(Loc V, Ψ (F′ M))` naturally in V and M": compose the equivalence for `F′` with the inverse of the one for `F`. Both are equivalences of bifunctors `Rᵒᵖ × D → Spc`, so the composite is one too. Correct.
2. "since the right adjoint of Loc is fully faithful (...), the Yoneda lemma gives `Ψ ∘ F ≃ Ψ ∘ F′`": the adjunction turns step 1 into `Maps(V, Loc^R Ψ F M) ≃ Maps(V, Loc^R Ψ F′ M)`, natural in `(V, M)`. Yoneda in `V` gives `Loc^R ∘ Ψ ∘ F ≃ Loc^R ∘ Ψ ∘ F′` as functors `D → R`. Since `Loc^R` is fully faithful ([GLC1] line 1452), composing with it is fully faithful on functor categories, so `Ψ ∘ F ≃ Ψ ∘ F′`. Correct. It needs naturality in `V` and `M` together, which property 2 gives. It does not use [GLC1] Thm. 1.2.4.
3. "On compact objects of `Dmod_½(Bun_G)`, F and F′ take bounded-below values, where Ψ is an equivalence (...), so F and F′ agree on compact objects": property 1 for each of `F` and `F′` puts the restrictions `F|_{D^c}` and `F′|_{D^c}` in the full subcategory `I^{>-∞}`. [GLC1] §1.6.3, lines 1820–1826: `Ψ_{Nilp,{0}}` "is t-exact and induces an equivalence $\IndCoh_\Nilp(\LS_\cG)^{>-\infty}\to \QCoh(\LS_\cG)^{>-\infty}$, see \cite[Proposition 4.4.5]{AG}". [ArinkinGaitsgory] Prop. 4.4.5 (`singsupp.tex` line 3158) gives the equivalence on `≥ 0` parts. With t-exactness and shifts it gives the equivalence on every `≥ -n` part, so on the union. So `Ψ` is fully faithful on `I^{>-∞}`, composing with it is fully faithful on `Fun(D^c, I^{>-∞})`, and step 2 restricted to `D^c` gives `F|_{D^c} ≃ F′|_{D^c}`. Correct. Only full faithfulness is used, not essential surjectivity. The bound may depend on the object, as property 1 allows.
4. "`Dmod_½(Bun_G)` is compactly generated (...), and continuous functors are determined by their restriction to compact objects (...), so F ≃ F′": [GLC1] §1.6.4 line 1831 ("is compactly generated (see \cite[Theorem 0.1.2]{DG1})"). For compactly generated `D`, `D ≃ Ind(D^c)`, and a continuous functor out of `D` is the left Kan extension of its restriction to `D^c`. So restriction is fully faithful on continuous functors, and step 3 extends to `F ≃ F′` as functors of ∞-categories. Correct. This is the paper's own argument for "uniquely characterized" in Cor. 1.6.5 (line 1836).
5. "a k-linear functor whose underlying functor of ∞-categories is an equivalence is an equivalence of DG categories (`Infinity.linearEquivalence`)": correctly stated. A DG category is a module over `Vect` in presentable stable ∞-categories ([AGKRRV] §0.9.4). The forgetful functors from modules to presentable categories, and from presentable categories to ∞-categories, are conservative. So the inverse of the underlying functor carries a unique continuous `k`-linear structure. The plan node `Infinity.linearEquivalence` says "equivalent to categorical full faithfulness and essential surjectivity", which is this statement once it is proved for the underlying functor. With step 4: if `F` is an equivalence of DG categories, its underlying functor is an equivalence, so the underlying functor of `F′` is one, so `F′` is an equivalence of DG categories, and the same with `F` and `F′` exchanged. The natural equivalence `F ≃ F′` need not be `k`-linear for this.

The other sentences of the `desc` are true. "By `Langlands.isLanglandsFunctor_iff_comp_psi_iso_coarse`, the functor 𝕃_G of [GLC1] Cor. 1.6.5 is one such functor": yes, by the "if" direction of the comparison. "its existence rests on [GLC1] Thm. 1.6.2": Cor. 1.6.5 combines Thm. 1.6.2 with §§1.6.3–1.6.4 (line 1830), so Thm. 1.6.2 is the new input; "rests on" does not claim it is the only one. "A theorem of the proof, not used by the statement": correct, the main theorem does not mention it. All seven `deps` exist in `plans/`.

The theorem is also what the independent assessment asked for ("an explicit future obligation for the whole-statement comparison, including compact-generation uniqueness and detection of equivalences in `DGCat`").
