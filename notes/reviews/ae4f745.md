## Notes on the points of the brief

**Point (i): the proof in `Groups.representationCategory_compactlyGenerated_rigid`.** I checked every step, and the argument is correct.
- *BH is QCA.* [DG2] Definition 1.1.8 asks for quasi-compactness, affine automorphism groups of geometric points, and classical inertia of finite presentation over the classical stack. The atlas pt → BH gives the first. The diagonal of BH is affine of finite type, because its fibres are torsors under H. The stabilizers are forms of H, and the inertia is a base change of the diagonal over a noetherian base. The plan's `Stacks.IsQCA` also asks for a schematic, quasi-separated, quasi-compact diagonal; an affine diagonal gives that. So the one sentence of the node is enough.
- *The unit and the finite-dimensional V.* Theorem 1.4.2 (i) is the continuity of Γ(BH, −), and the source itself rephrases it as "the object O_𝒴 ∈ QCoh(𝒴) is compact" (`Finiteness.tex` 1851–1852). The step for V is the first half of the proof of Corollary 1.4.3 (1864–1865).
- *Generation.* π^* is conservative, because QCoh(BH) is the totalization of QCoh of the Čech nerve of π, and an object of a totalization is zero when its term in degree 0 is zero. π is affine, so π_* is continuous and satisfies base change and the projection formula; hence π_*π^*M ≃ M ⊗ π_*k = M ⊗ O_H. π_* is t-exact, so O_H is in the heart, and there it is the regular comodule. The t-structure of QCoh(BH) is compatible with filtered colimits (π^* is t-exact, conservative and continuous), so the union of the finite-dimensional subcomodules W is a filtered colimit in QCoh(BH). Then π^*M = Γ(BH, π_*π^*M) = Γ(BH, colim M ⊗ W) = colim Γ(BH, M ⊗ W) = colim Hom(W^∨, M); the third step is the compact unit. If all maps from shifts of finite-dimensional representations to M are zero, each Hom(W^∨, M) is zero, so π^*M = 0 and M = 0.
- *Every compact object has a tensor dual.* It follows in two ways. The node's way: in a compactly generated stable category the compact objects are the thick closure of the generators, and dualizable objects are closed under shifts, cones and retracts. The source's way: Corollary 1.4.3 says that on a QCA stack compact is the same as perfect, and perfect is the same as dualizable (1859–1872).
- *The hypotheses.* Characteristic zero is needed: for Z/p or for the additive group in characteristic p the trivial representation has cohomology in infinitely many degrees, so the unit is not compact ([DG2] discusses the additive group in characteristic p at `Finiteness.tex` 1502–1503, for a related failure). Finite type is what the QCA theorem needs. It is not necessary for the conclusion (a pro-reductive group also has it), but it is the right hypothesis for this theorem and for its users. [DG2] works over any field of characteristic zero (807–808), as the node says.
- *The other route.* [DG2] Corollary 1.4.11 gives generation of QCoh(BH) by the coherent heart, which is the finite-dimensional representations; BH is classical, so it is eventually coconnective and noetherian. So the statement of the node is in the source, and the node's direct proof is shorter than the source's (a stratification argument). The printed proof of Lemma 2.6.3 is one sentence (2497), so citing the lemma alone would not be enough; the direct argument is the better plan.

**Point (ii): the two forgetful functors.**
- *Well defined.* The trivial H-torsor on the relative formal disc gives a section of the spread classifying stack over (X^I)_dR for every nonempty I, compatible with the diagonal maps. Pullback along it is a continuous, symmetric monoidal, QCoh((X^I)_dR)-linear functor to QCoh((X^I)_dR) = Dmod(X^I). The collision-side Oblv_{X^I} is the source's ([RaskinCPSI] §6.21). It is conservative: each term Dmod(U(p)) is dualizable, so Rep(H)^{⊗K} ⊗ Dmod(U(p)) is a category of continuous functors into Rep(H)^{⊗K}, and composing with a conservative functor is conservative.
- *Not circular.* The dependencies are acyclic: `regularObject` does not depend on `equivCollisionLimit`, and `equivCollisionLimit` and `linearFunctorEquivComodule` depend on `regularObject`. The text of `regularObject` refers forward to the comparison for the transport. Gap 4 removes this forward reference: the geometric right adjoint and regular object are defined directly.
- *The "intertwines" clause is a real statement.* It compares two functors that are defined independently. The construction of the canonical functor of Lemma 9.8.1 supports it (`cpsii-excerpt.tex` 205–209): the component of the comparison at (I ↠ J ↠ K) is pullback along constant torsors, the component of Oblv_{X^I} is pullback along pt → (BH)^K, and their composite is pullback along the trivial torsor over U(p). These agree for all Σ, and the limit of the Dmod(U(p)) is Dmod(X^I). The proof of the lemma in Appendix A.5 does not mention the forgetful functor. It goes through the gauge-form presentation, where the trivial torsor is the zero form; so the proof must also show that the equivalence it builds is the canonical functor. The source leaves this to the reader. Gap 1 makes the node say which functor is meant.
- *What the users get.* `linearFunctorEquivComodule` needs the object of the geometric category on which functors are evaluated, and the coalgebra in Dmod(X^I) that acts on D. The coalgebra is Oblv of the regular object; it is the same on both sides once the forgetful functors are intertwined. The map F ↦ F(O_{H,X^I}) with its coaction is canonical: the coaction comes from the unit of the adjunction and the linearity of the right adjoint.
